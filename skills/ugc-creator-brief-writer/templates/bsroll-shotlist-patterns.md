# B-Roll Shot List Patterns

The b-roll shot list tells the creator what to film *besides* the talking head. Strong b-roll is what makes UGC look professional; weak or missing b-roll is what makes it look like a friend filming a friend.

Every brief includes 5-10 specific shots. Shots should be product-relevant, vary in framing, and follow a pattern the creator can shoot in one session.

## Why 5-10

Too few (1-3 shots): the video relies on talking-head, which looks low-effort.
Too many (15+): the creator skips half of them or rushes the shoot and quality drops.
5-10: enough variety for a 30-second edit without overwhelming the shoot.

## Shot list structure

For each shot, specify:
- **Shot number** (so the creator can check them off)
- **Shot description** (one sentence — what's in frame, framing distance, action)
- **Why it matters** (one phrase — what this shot is doing for the edit; helps the creator prioritize if time is short)
- **Reference** (optional — a link to an existing video or photo that captures the framing, OR a 1-line composition note)

Example shot list entry:

> **Shot 3: Close-up of the dripper mid-pour, water streaming from kettle to dripper, slow motion if possible.**
> _Why it matters:_ this is the "money shot" — the visual that sells the product. Should appear around second 10-15 in the edit.
> _Reference:_ Like in this video [link], but with your kettle and counter.

## Universal shot pattern (the 5-shot foundation)

Almost every product UGC benefits from these 5 shot types:

### Shot 1: Establishing shot — product in context
- Wide shot showing the product where it lives (the kitchen counter for a coffee dripper, the bathroom shelf for a skincare product, the desk for a productivity tool)
- Frames the scene: the audience sees where this fits into life

### Shot 2: Hero close-up — product detail
- Tight on the product, ideally with a hand for scale and "in use" energy
- Shows craftsmanship / materials / build quality

### Shot 3: In-use / demonstration
- The product actively being used — brewing, applying, opening, wearing, etc.
- This is usually the "money shot" — the visual that does the most selling

### Shot 4: Before / after OR reaction
- Either the result of using the product (face glowing, coffee in mug, organized counter) — shot as a hero result
- OR the creator's face reacting to the result (delight, surprise, satisfaction — kept natural, not acted)

### Shot 5: Talking-head in context
- Creator speaking to camera, but with the product visible in the same frame
- Used for opening hook delivery and closing CTAs

These 5 shots alone can carry a 30-second UGC video. The remaining 3-5 shots (in a typical 8-shot list) are product-specific additions.

## Product-specific shot patterns (additions to the 5-shot foundation)

### For consumables (food, drink, supplements, beauty)

Add:
- **Ingredient close-up** (label, ingredient list, materials)
- **First-use / unboxing** (creator opening the package — wraps the "I got this product" moment into the content)
- **Application detail** (close-up of the product being put on / consumed / measured)

### For tools / gadgets

Add:
- **Mechanism close-up** (the moving part, the click, the snap into place)
- **Comparison shot** (the buyer's product next to an alternative — but watch for trademark issues)
- **End-state shot** (the result of using the tool — clean counter, sharpened knife, organized space)

### For apparel / accessories

Add:
- **Outfit shot** (creator wearing the product as part of a styled look)
- **Detail shot** (stitching, hardware, fabric, fit specifics)
- **Multiple-angle shot** (creator turning to show the product from front, side, back)

### For home goods / decor

Add:
- **Room-context shot** (product in its placed environment)
- **Texture / material close-up** (surface detail)
- **Lighting variation** (how the product looks in morning vs evening light, if relevant)

### For tech / electronics

Add:
- **Screen / interface close-up** (over-the-shoulder of someone using the device)
- **Setup shot** (powering on, connecting, the first moment of use)
- **End-result shot** (the output of the device — photo, recording, charged battery indicator)

### For kids' / parenting products

Add:
- **Child interaction shot** (kid actually using it, with permission and FTC-appropriate framing)
- **Parent reaction** (the relief / pride moment)
- **Setup shot** (a parent assembling, washing, prepping the product)

### For services / digital products

Add:
- **Screen-recording shot** (of the app, site, or platform in use)
- **Workspace context** (the creator's setup where they use the service)
- **Result shot** (what they made / accomplished with the service)

## Shot variation principles

Strong shot lists vary across these dimensions:

- **Framing distance** (mix wide, medium, close-up)
- **Camera angle** (mix straight-on, slight overhead, slight worm's-eye)
- **Static vs motion** (mix still shots with shots that have motion — pour, wear, walk-through)
- **With creator vs without** (some shots have the creator in frame, some are pure product)
- **Lighting** (where natural, mix bright/window light with softer / atmospheric)

A shot list of 8 shots that are all close-up + static + no creator is a bad list — it'll produce a monotonous edit. Force variety.

---

## Shot list quality criteria

Every shot description should:

- **Be specific enough that the creator knows exactly what to film** ("close-up of the cork base on a wooden surface, top-down angle, with the dripper sitting upright" — NOT "a close-up of the product")
- **Be technically achievable on a phone** (most UGC creators shoot with iPhones; don't ask for shots that require professional gear unless the creator's portfolio shows they have it)
- **Be shootable in one or two sessions** (don't ask for "shot during golden hour, shot at noon, shot at sunset, shot at night" — creators won't actually do four different light sessions for one video)

---

## How shot lists differ by brief type

### Organic UGC

- 8-10 shots typical
- Can include creative / experimental framings
- Creator has more latitude on composition
- A "wild card" shot ("one shot you think captures the vibe — your call") is allowed and welcomed

### Paid UGC (Spark / Whitelisted)

- 6-8 shots typical (tighter)
- Specific framings (paid edits have less room for ambiguity — they need to convert)
- All shots must support a clear conversion narrative
- "Wild card" shots discouraged — every frame should be intentional

### Affiliate

- 6-8 shots typical
- Must include at least one shot of the on-screen code or link visibility (per affiliate requirements)
- Other shots follow the organic pattern

---

## Common pitfalls

- **Too many talking-head shots** — videos that are 80% talking head feel low-effort
- **No close-ups** — flat compositions; nothing to feature
- **Generic shot descriptions** ("a beauty shot of the product") — creators improvise badly when not given specifics
- **Shots that require gear most creators don't have** — drone shots, dolly moves, multi-camera setups
- **No shot variation** — all shots from the same angle, same distance, same lighting

---

## Output formatting in the brief

Shot list appears in the brief as a numbered list with the structure above. For campaign mode briefs, the master brief has the full shot list; per-creator briefs assign 5-6 shots per creator (so two creators aren't shooting the same shot list — variety across the campaign matters).
