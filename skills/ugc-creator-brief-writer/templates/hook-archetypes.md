# Hook Archetypes Library

Every UGC brief generates 10 hooks across 5 angle archetypes (2 hooks per archetype). The archetypes test fundamentally different *arguments* — not minor word swaps — so the buyer learns whether their audience responds more to pain, proof, transformation, curiosity, or contrarian framings.

The 10 hooks get assigned across creators in campaign mode (so no two are shooting the same hook) or presented as a menu for the creator to pick in single mode.

## Why 5 archetypes

UGC ad performance lives or dies on the hook — it determines whether the viewer watches 2 seconds or 30. Most "test 10 variations" attempts test 10 versions of the same hook, which produces noisy data. Testing 5 distinct archetypes (with 2 hooks per archetype) gives the buyer real signal on which *angle* their audience responds to.

## Archetype 1: Problem-aware

**The hook:** Name a pain the audience already feels, in their own words.

**Best for:** problem-solution products (supplements, organization tools, productivity, comfort items), audiences that already know they have the problem.

**Hook pattern:** Open with the felt pain. The audience nods internally.

**Hook examples by product:**

> **For a posture-correction brace:**
> - "Your back hurts every time you stand up from your desk and it's only getting worse."
> - "If you've been telling yourself the slouch is fine — it isn't, and your future spine is sending receipts."

> **For a sleep supplement:**
> - "It's 2am and you're still scrolling because you can't fall asleep without sitting up first."
> - "The 3-4am wake-up that ruins the next day starts hours earlier than you think."

> **For a meal-prep container set:**
> - "It's Sunday and you've already started dreading Monday's lunch because you didn't prep."
> - "Your fridge is full of leftovers you're never going to eat because you can't see them."

**Writing rules:**
- Use the audience's own words (pulled from reviews, comments, customer service tickets when possible)
- Be specific (not "your back hurts" but "your back hurts every time you stand up from your desk")
- Don't over-agitate — one sentence of pain is enough; more reads exploitative

**Don'ts:**
- Don't pretend a pain the audience doesn't actually feel
- Don't open with "Are you tired of...?" — over-used to the point of self-parody
- Don't lead with the product

## Archetype 2: Social-proof

**The hook:** Reference credibility — review count, press, expert backing, or "everyone's doing this."

**Best for:** newer brands competing in mature categories, products where shoppers default to skeptical, B2B / professional / health categories where trust is required.

**Hook pattern:** Open with the proof. Earned attention before the pitch.

**Hook examples by product:**

> **For a $400 chef's knife:**
> - "Used by 8 of NYC's top kitchens — and now you can have one."
> - "1,200 chefs reviewed every knife under $500 and put this one at #1."

> **For an eco-cleaning subscription:**
> - "Wirecutter just named this the best non-toxic cleaner of the year."
> - "12,000 households switched. Here's why."

> **For a parenting course:**
> - "Pediatricians have been quietly recommending this approach for years. Now there's a free class."
> - "300 parents tried it for 30 days. Here's what they reported."

**Writing rules:**
- Numbers should be specific (1,247 reviews > "lots of reviews")
- Credentials should be relevant (a chef quote for cookware, not for skincare)
- Don't fabricate — overpromise here kills brand credibility on contact

**Don'ts:**
- Don't lead with vague claims ("trusted by thousands")
- Don't use credentials the brand doesn't actually have
- Don't lead with celebrity endorsements unless the celebrity is genuinely associated (paid mentions read as paid)

## Archetype 3: Transformation

**The hook:** Show the after-state. Paint the future version of the audience.

**Best for:** aspirational/lifestyle products (beauty, fitness, home decor, courses, productivity tools).

**Hook pattern:** Sensory snapshot of the desired state, no setup needed.

**Hook examples by product:**

> **For a skincare line:**
> - "Wake up to skin that's soft, even, and feels calm — without a 12-step routine."
> - "I touched my face at 3pm today and didn't find a single oil slick. Three weeks ago that was impossible."

> **For a productivity app:**
> - "What if your inbox was empty by 9:30am and stayed that way?"
> - "Imagine closing your laptop at 5:01 because everything that mattered today is done."

> **For a fitness program:**
> - "Walk up the stairs at the end of the day without thinking about it for the first time in years."
> - "Coming home from the airport with your own carry-on, no help, no rest stops."

**Writing rules:**
- Make the after-state sensory and specific (not "feel great" but "wake up without back pain")
- Anchor it in a moment, not a feeling (a specific scene > a vague mood)
- The after-state should feel achievable — not impossible/aspirational beyond reach

**Don'ts:**
- Don't promise overnight transformation
- Don't use stock-image-style abstractions ("a better you," "the life you deserve")
- Don't paint a transformation that legally constitutes a claim (medical, financial)

## Archetype 4: Curiosity gap

**The hook:** Set up an unanswered question or unexpected setup the viewer needs the rest of the video to resolve.

**Best for:** products that benefit from explanation, categories crowded with similar-sounding ads, anything where the value isn't immediately obvious.

**Hook pattern:** Open with something incomplete. The viewer keeps watching to get the resolution.

**Hook examples by product:**

> **For a kitchen gadget:**
> - "I've been chopping onions wrong for 10 years and didn't know until this arrived."
> - "There's one thing missing from every dish you make and it's not seasoning."

> **For a sleep supplement:**
> - "The reason you're tired even after 8 hours has nothing to do with how long you slept."
> - "I tried every sleep hack on TikTok for 30 days. Only one actually worked."

> **For a budget app:**
> - "I tracked every expense for 90 days and was wrong about where 60% of my money went."
> - "There's a category on your bank statement adding up to $300 a month and you don't know what it is."

**Writing rules:**
- The gap must resolve in the video — never leave the viewer hanging without payoff
- The setup should be specific (vague "you'll be shocked" gets ignored)
- Most effective with educational / story-led content; less so with pure product showcases

**Don'ts:**
- Don't manufacture mystery where there isn't any
- Don't promise more than the video can deliver
- Don't use clickbait-style cliffhangers — they perform short-term but burn brand trust

## Archetype 5: Contrarian

**The hook:** Challenge a common belief in the category. The audience has to listen to defend their existing view or to learn a better one.

**Best for:** categories with strong prevailing wisdom that the brand is positioned against (slow vs fast, expensive vs cheap, traditional vs new, single-use vs reusable).

**Hook pattern:** Stake out a position that contradicts what most people in the category say.

**Hook examples by product:**

> **For a slow-cooked supplement:**
> - "Your morning multivitamin is doing less than the bottle suggests. Here's what to take instead."
> - "Stop buying gummy vitamins — they're 60% sugar and your body knows it."

> **For a reusable coffee filter:**
> - "Paper filters are quietly the most wasteful thing in your kitchen."
> - "Espresso machines are over. Hand-brewed pour-over is the actually-good way."

> **For a no-meal-prep system:**
> - "Meal-prepping for the week makes you eat worse, not better. Here's why."
> - "The 'just eat the rainbow' nutrition advice is wrong for most people."

**Writing rules:**
- The contrary position must be defensible (the brand should actually believe it)
- Be specific about what you're contradicting (not "wellness influencers are wrong" but "wellness influencers tell you to do X — here's why that fails")
- Avoid attacking specific competitors by name (TOS issues + legal risk)

**Don'ts:**
- Don't be contrarian for its own sake — the position has to be substantive
- Don't be mean-spirited about the prevailing view (alienates viewers who hold it)
- Don't make claims you can't back up (contrarian positions get fact-checked harder)

## Hook generation per brief

The skill always produces 10 hooks (2 per archetype). The 2 hooks per archetype should be meaningfully different from each other, not minor word swaps.

For brand-voice alignment: read `config.brand_voice` and adjust register. A bold/punchy brand's contrarian hook reads differently from a calm/considered brand's contrarian hook — same archetype, different voice.

For product-specific tuning: pull real customer language from reviews when available. A hook that uses the audience's actual phrases outperforms a hook that uses the brand's marketing phrases by a wide margin.

---

## Hook formatting in the brief

In the .docx brief, hooks are presented as a table with these columns:

| # | Archetype | Hook (spoken line) | Recommended on-screen text | Notes for the creator |

The "Notes for the creator" column suggests delivery direction: "deliver flat, casual, no acting voice" or "punch the first word for impact" or "pair with a slight head-shake on the contrarian setup."

This lets the creator pick the 2-3 hooks that fit their on-camera style and lean into them.

---

## When the buyer asks for "more hooks"

If a creator's brief needs more than 10 hooks (very large campaign, lots of variants), generate up to 20 across the same 5 archetypes (4 per archetype). Don't go beyond 20 — past that, hooks blur together and the buyer can't actually deploy them all.

If the buyer asks for hooks in a specific archetype only ("give me 5 contrarian hooks"), honor it. Single-archetype briefs work for buyers running a focused test on one angle.
