# FTC Disclosure + Usage Rights

For paid UGC and affiliate content, FTC disclosure is legally required and usage rights need to be explicit in the brief. This file defines the language and structure. The brief should reproduce these sections close to verbatim — they protect both the buyer and the creator.

This is not legal advice. Buyers should run final terms past their attorney for high-stakes or high-volume creator programs. The language here reflects FTC guidance current as of brief generation and is the safe default.

---

## When FTC disclosure is required

| Scenario | Disclosure required? |
|---|---|
| Buyer ships free product, creator decides what to post | Yes (per FTC: "material connection" exists) |
| Buyer pays flat fee, content lives on creator's account | Yes |
| Buyer pays flat fee, content lives only on buyer's owned channels | No (buyer's own marketing content) |
| Spark Ads — buyer boosts creator's organic post | Yes (the post is endorsed content) |
| Whitelisted ads — buyer runs through creator's handle | Yes |
| Affiliate — creator earns commission | Yes (per FTC: material connection exists) |
| Creator was paid for the content but the buyer is reposting on their own account only | No (buyer's marketing content) |

**Default rule:** if the creator's audience sees the content on the creator's own channel and the creator received compensation (money, product, commission), disclosure is required.

## FTC-compliant disclosure language

The FTC requires "clear and conspicuous" disclosure. That means:

- Visible in the video itself (not buried in the caption)
- Above the fold (not under a "more" expansion)
- In language a typical viewer understands

### For paid UGC content

Required in **both** the video itself and the caption:

**In the video** — text overlay starting at the very beginning of the video:

```
"Paid partnership with [@brand_handle]"
```

OR (also acceptable):

```
"#ad"
```

OR (most explicit, often recommended):

```
"This is a paid partnership with [@brand_handle]. I was compensated to make this video."
```

The text overlay should appear in frame for at least the first 2 seconds and stay visible (or reappear) throughout the video.

**In the caption** — first line:

```
Paid partnership with @brand_handle. #ad
```

Both Instagram and TikTok also have a native "Paid partnership" tool that adds an automated tag at the top of the post. Use it. The native tag is not a substitute for the in-video disclosure but supplements it.

### For affiliate content

Affiliate content requires disclosure of the affiliate relationship. Acceptable language:

**In the video** — text overlay:

```
"#affiliate" or "Affiliate link below"
```

**In the caption / pinned comment:**

```
"This post contains affiliate links. I earn a small commission on sales — at no extra cost to you. #affiliate"
```

If the creator is using a discount code, the on-screen text should also say "Code visible below" or "Code in bio."

### For product-seeding (free product, no payment)

Disclosure is still required. Acceptable language:

**In the video:** "Gifted by [@brand_handle]" or "#gifted"

**In the caption:** "[Brand] sent me this — sharing because I genuinely like it. #gifted"

## Platform-specific disclosure features

### TikTok

- **Branded Content Toggle:** when creating the post, the creator toggles "I am promoting a brand or product." This must be on for any paid or gifted content. The toggle auto-adds a disclosure label at the top.
- **Spark Ads disclosure:** when the buyer runs the post as a Spark Ad, TikTok's ad UI automatically adds an "Ad" badge. The in-video and caption disclosure are still required on top of this.

### Instagram

- **Paid Partnership Tool:** available in the post settings. When toggled on, the creator can tag the brand as a paid partner, which auto-adds "Paid partnership with [brand]" above the post. Required for paid; recommended even for gifted.
- **Stories paid partnership:** same tool exists for Stories. Tag the brand.

### YouTube

- **Paid Promotion Toggle:** at video upload, YouTube asks "Does this video contain paid promotion?" The creator must toggle yes. This adds an automatic "Includes paid promotion" overlay at the start.
- **In-video and description disclosure are still required** on top of this.

The brief should specify in the deliverables section: "Use the native [platform] paid-partnership toggle when posting" so creators don't forget.

## Usage rights paragraph (for Paid UGC)

The usage rights paragraph in a paid brief defines what the buyer can do with the footage. Without explicit rights, the creator retains ownership and the buyer technically can't run ads.

Standard usage rights language to include:

```
Usage rights:

By delivering this content, you grant [Brand Name] a non-exclusive, paid-up,
worldwide license to use, modify, edit, and distribute the content for the
purpose of paid advertising on Meta (Facebook + Instagram), TikTok, and YouTube
for a period of [X months from delivery date].

This license includes:
- The right to run the original video as a paid ad on the platforms named above
- The right to create up to [N] cutdowns or variants from the source footage,
  for use in the same channels
- The right to use individual frames or clips in static / image-based ads
- The right to add captions, voiceover, or other editorial modifications

This license does NOT include:
- Use of your name, likeness, or content outside the platforms named above
- Use of your content in print, TV, OOH, or any non-digital channel
- Use of your content beyond [X months] without a separate agreement
- Sublicensing to third parties

You retain ownership of the underlying creative work and may continue to use it
on your own channels indefinitely.

Compensation: [flat fee for production + usage agreed]
```

Adjust the values in brackets (`[X months]`, `[N]`, etc.) per the buyer's specific deal. If the buyer doesn't know what to put for usage duration, recommend 3 months as a default — long enough to test paid performance, short enough to keep costs reasonable.

For whitelisted/allowlisted ads (where the buyer runs ads through the creator's handle), add this paragraph:

```
Allowlisting:

This agreement also includes allowlisting access. You will enable [Brand Name]
to run ads through your @[creator_handle] handle on Meta and/or TikTok for the
same [X month] period. We will only run ads using the content delivered under
this agreement; we will not post organic content from your handle.

To enable allowlisting:
- Meta: Settings > Business Tools > Add [Brand Name]'s Business Manager as a
  partner with "Ads" permission
- TikTok: Settings > Spark Ads > Authorize [Brand Name]'s ad account
```

## Brand-safety guardrails (paid UGC)

For paid UGC briefs, include this section so creators don't accidentally produce content that gets the ad rejected by Meta or TikTok auto-review.

### Things to avoid in paid UGC

**Personal attributes language** — Meta's policy prohibits implying the viewer has a personal attribute. Even framed positively, these phrases cause rejection:

- ❌ "If you're overweight..."
- ❌ "As a [skin tone / condition] person..."
- ❌ "For people with [X medical condition]..."
- ❌ "If you've ever felt insecure about..."
- ✅ Reframe to: "If you're looking for..." / "Anyone who wants..."

**Before-and-after with body/face shots** — heavily restricted for beauty, fitness, weight-loss categories. Allowed only with specific guardrails (must be "subtle," include a typical disclaimer).

**Medical / health claims** — even mild ones trigger flags:

- ❌ "Clears acne," "stops migraines," "fixes hormones"
- ❌ "Doctor approved," "dermatologist recommended" (unless verifiable + cited)
- ✅ Reframe to: "May support [outcome]," "designed for [use case]"

**Profanity** — even bleeped — TikTok is stricter than Meta. Default: no profanity.

**Misleading "results"** — extreme transformations, "lose 30 lbs in a month" — autoreject.

**References to other platforms** — "Follow me on Instagram" in a TikTok ad gets flagged. "Subscribe to my YouTube" in a Reel gets flagged. Don't cross-promote in paid.

**Crisis topics referenced casually** — war, addiction, mental health crises — even sympathetically, can trigger sensitive-content flags.

**Currency or financial claims** — "I made $5000 last week," "Pay off your debt fast" — financial categories have their own strict rules.

### The brief's section on brand safety

In the brief, summarize these as creator-friendly bullets:

```
Brand safety — please avoid:

1. Saying or implying the viewer has a body type, condition, or attribute
   (Meta's policy bans this even when framed positively). Use "if you want X"
   instead of "if you're [type of person]."

2. Medical or health claims about the product, even mild ones ("clears,"
   "stops," "fixes," "doctor approved"). Use "designed for" or "may support."

3. Profanity, even bleeped. TikTok's auto-review flags bleeps as profanity.

4. Mentioning other platforms ("Follow me on Insta," "Subscribe to my
   YouTube"). Platforms downrank cross-references in paid content.

5. Before/after body shots, weight references, transformation timelines.

6. Casual references to addiction, crisis, mental health, war.

If your video gets rejected by Meta or TikTok auto-review, we'll lose the ad
spend and have to scrap the content. Following the list above keeps us both
in the clear.
```

This framing protects the creator (they're not getting yelled at for compliance) and gets the result (ads that actually run).
