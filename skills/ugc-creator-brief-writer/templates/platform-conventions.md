# Platform Conventions

Four platforms supported. Each has its own format conventions, hook timing, caption rules, and music guidance. The brief should specify per-platform notes wherever they differ.

When a brief covers multiple platforms, group platform-specific notes at the bottom of each section ("If shooting for Reels, also..." / "For YouTube Shorts, instead of...").

---

## TikTok

The original UGC playground. Sets the conventions other platforms borrow.

### Format specs

- **Aspect ratio:** 9:16 vertical
- **Length:** 7-90 seconds; sweet spot is 15-30s for ads, 30-60s for organic. Long-form (1-10 min) is growing but not the default for UGC.
- **Frame rate:** 30fps minimum, 60fps preferred (smoother for fast-paced edits)
- **Audio:** trending sounds are critical for organic; licensed audio required for paid (TikTok's commercial music library)

### Hook timing

- **First 1-2 seconds are everything** — TikTok's algorithm measures "stop rate" within the first 2 seconds. If users scroll past, the video never gets boosted.
- Hook = visual + text overlay + spoken line simultaneously
- Spoken hooks should land in the first second of audio
- Text-overlay hooks should be on screen by frame 1

### Caption conventions

- Keep captions short (≤150 chars). Long captions get truncated by the "more" overlay.
- Hashtags work but don't overload (3-5 max). Stuffing hurts reach.
- Mention the creator's collaborator or sponsor: "@brandhandle" — Spark Ads require this anyway

### Music guidance for the brief

- **Organic:** trending sound from TikTok's Creator Music library (the creator picks based on what's trending the day they post)
- **Paid (Spark/whitelisted):** must use TikTok's Commercial Music Library or original/licensed audio. Brief should specify "use Commercial Music Library only — no trending sounds for paid"

### TikTok-specific don'ts

- Don't say "Instagram" or "Reels" — TikTok downranks cross-platform references
- Don't include external URLs in the video (they're allowed in bio, not on-screen)
- Don't use a horizontal aspect ratio uncut (the algorithm penalizes black bars)
- Don't use stock-feeling music — TikTok organic relies on platform-native sound

### Hook style for TikTok

Lean into:
- POV setups ("POV: you finally found the [thing] that...")
- Direct address ("If you've ever tried to [problem]...")
- Curiosity gaps ("I've been doing [common thing] wrong for years")
- Reaction-style ("They didn't tell me [X] when I bought this")

## Instagram Reels

Similar to TikTok in format; different culture and engagement patterns.

### Format specs

- **Aspect ratio:** 9:16 vertical
- **Length:** 15-90 seconds; sweet spot is 30-60s (longer than TikTok's sweet spot)
- **Frame rate:** 30fps minimum
- **Audio:** trending audio still matters for organic; for paid, licensed audio required

### Hook timing

- **First 3 seconds matter** — Instagram is slightly less brutal than TikTok on stop rate. Hooks can build slightly longer.
- Strong visual + text overlay still wins
- The hook line can land in second 1-2 (not strictly second 1 like TikTok)

### Caption conventions

- Longer captions are fine on Instagram (up to 2200 chars) and often outperform short ones — the algorithm rewards engagement, and longer captions get more comments
- Use line breaks for readability
- Hashtags: 3-10 work, can include 1-2 niche-specific ones
- @-tag the brand and any collaborator

### Music guidance

- **Organic:** trending Reels audio + Spark licensed audio both work
- **Paid:** Licensed audio or original audio only. No trending sounds (rights issue).

### Reels-specific notes

- Cross-posted TikTok videos often have the TikTok watermark visible — Instagram down-ranks these. Brief should specify "shoot natively for Reels or use a tool to scrub the TikTok watermark before posting."
- "Trial Reels" (Instagram's feature to test content with non-followers) is useful for paid UGC's first test before scaling.
- Captions should consider both Reels feed and Explore tab — explore tab is shorter-attention

### Hook style for Reels

Lean into:
- Aesthetic/lifestyle setups (Reels skews more polished than TikTok)
- Story-led hooks ("Here's what happened when I tried...")
- Aspirational framings (Reels audience leans toward "I want my life to look like this")
- Educational hooks ("Three things I wish I knew before...")

## Instagram Stories

Different from Reels. More casual, time-limited, often raw.

### Format specs

- **Aspect ratio:** 9:16 vertical
- **Length:** 15 seconds per clip (Stories auto-cut longer videos into 15s segments)
- **Frame rate:** 30fps
- **Audio:** music sticker for trending, voice-over works, often the audio is just ambient

### Hook timing

- **First second is critical** (people tap through Stories aggressively)
- Hook is often text-overlay only (Stories are often watched muted, in motion)
- Stories with sound expect the user has tapped through and is paying attention

### Caption conventions

- No caption field in Stories — text overlays do the captioning job
- Polls, questions, sliders are heavily encouraged (Stories' interactive stickers boost engagement and signal to the algorithm)
- Hashtag stickers work but should be sparing

### Stories-specific notes

- Stories live 24 hours. For a UGC brief, this matters: the buyer wants permanent content (Reels/posts) for longer-lived assets. Stories are good for time-sensitive promos, launches, or as soft-launch tests before posting the same content as Reels.
- Multiple-clip Stories sequences (5-10 clips strung together) are a format. Brief should specify "single clip" or "sequence of N clips."
- Story Highlights are user-curated saved Stories on a profile. If the buyer wants the content to live forever, they should plan to add it to a Highlight (note in the brief).

### Hook style for Stories

Lean into:
- Personal, conversational ("I'm gonna show you something...")
- Interactive prompts ("Tap if you've ever...")
- Time-of-day context ("It's 7am and I'm just getting up")
- POV / first-person ("Watch what I do every morning")

## YouTube Shorts

Same vertical format but very different discovery + culture from TikTok.

### Format specs

- **Aspect ratio:** 9:16 vertical
- **Length:** up to 60 seconds (YouTube's hard cap; sweet spot 30-60s)
- **Frame rate:** 30fps minimum, 60fps preferred
- **Audio:** YouTube has its own audio library and royalty-free music tools

### Hook timing

- **First 3-5 seconds matter** — YouTube Shorts is less ruthless than TikTok on stop rate. Slightly longer hook setups are OK.
- Spoken hooks land more naturally (Shorts viewers are more sound-on than TikTok scrollers)
- Text-overlay is still useful but less essential

### Caption / metadata conventions

- **Title:** highly important (it's effectively the SEO field — YouTube Search indexes Short titles)
- **Description:** can include links, channel branding, related links
- **Tags:** still meaningful for Shorts (less so than YouTube long-form, but they help)
- **Hashtag in title:** "#Shorts" is recommended in the title to help YouTube categorize

### Shorts-specific notes

- YouTube Shorts has a much longer tail than TikTok or Reels. A Short can keep getting views weeks or months after posting.
- Subscriber-driven: Shorts that drive channel subscriptions are favored by the algorithm. The brief can note that the creator should include a clear CTA to subscribe to their channel (even though the buyer is paying for the brand content, the creator subscribing benefit is the trade).
- "Subscribe to [my channel]" CTAs work in Shorts; "Follow me on [other platform]" don't

### Hook style for Shorts

Lean into:
- Educational framings (Shorts viewers are slightly more "I want to learn something" than TikTok)
- Strong visual setups that promise resolution
- Statement-of-fact hooks ("The reason your [thing] keeps breaking is...")
- Title-driven hooks (the title carries the keyword, the visual hooks the viewer in the first 2 seconds)

## Cross-platform: when the brief covers multiple platforms

If the buyer's brief covers, say, TikTok + Reels + Stories, the structure of the brief stays the same, but each section gets per-platform notes where conventions diverge.

For example, the Music section might read:

> **Music guidance**
>
> - **TikTok:** trending sound from Creator Music (organic) or Commercial Music Library (Spark Ads). Avoid trending sounds for paid.
> - **Reels:** trending Reels audio or licensed track for organic; licensed only for paid.
> - **Stories:** music sticker for trending, voice-over OK, often ambient audio.
> - **YouTube Shorts:** YouTube's audio library or original/licensed.

The brief stays a single document but reads as multi-platform-aware. Avoid splitting into four separate briefs unless the buyer specifically wants per-platform documents.

When the buyer has assigned the same creator to deliver content for 2+ platforms, the brief should specify deliverables explicitly: "1 TikTok (30s), 1 Reels (30s, same content adjusted for Reels aesthetic), 1 set of 3 Story clips (15s each)."
