# Single Organic TikTok Brief — Reference Example

Fictional brief used to show structure. Never reproduce verbatim.

**Product:** River Stone Ceramic Pour-Over
**Brief type:** Organic UGC (buyer will repost to their own brand TikTok)
**Platform:** TikTok
**Creator:** One creator, single brief

The ask
We're producing a single 30-second TikTok showing the River Stone pour-over in your kitchen

Product context
The River Stone is a hand-thrown ceramic pour-over coffee dripper made in our 800-square-foot

Who this is for
Slow-morning people. Apartment dwellers who don't want countertop-hog machines.
Coffee lovers who like one careful cup over three rushed ones. Skews 25-40, design-conscious.

Tone of voice
Warm, slightly contemplative, not performative. Think: a friend showing you their morning, not

B-ROLL SHOT LIST (8 shots)
══════════════════════════════════════════════════

Shot 1
Wide shot of the pour-over sitting on your kitchen counter, morning light visible from a window.
Why it matters: Establishing shot — sets the scene of where this lives in your day.

Shot 2
Close-up of the cork base on the wooden countertop, slight overhead angle.
Why it matters: Shows the craftsmanship + the cork detail that makes it stable.

Shot 3
Hands lifting the kettle to pour into the dripper — water streaming. Slow-mo if possible.
Why it matters: This is the money shot. Lands at the visual peak of the video.
Reference: Like in any good coffee Reel — the steady spiral pour.

Shot 4
The mesh filter close-up — separate from the dripper, on a wooden surface.
Why it matters: Sells the "no paper" detail that's a big differentiator.

Shot 5
The finished cup of coffee in your hand, with the dripper visible behind it on the counter.
Why it matters: After-state. The reason you made the video.

Shot 6
Talking-head: you in your kitchen with the dripper visible on the counter behind you.
Why it matters: For hook delivery and any closing thought.

Shot 7
The dripper in the top rack of an open dishwasher.
Why it matters: Subtly sells the "dishwasher safe" benefit without making it explicit.

Shot 8
Detail shot of the stoneware texture — close enough to see the hand-thrown imperfections.
Why it matters: "Made by hand" claim becomes visible — earns the brand story.

══════════════════════════════════════════════════
DO'S AND DON'TS
══════════════════════════════════════════════════

Do
- Shoot in your actual kitchen — the lived-in look is what we want
- Use natural morning light if at all possible
- Show the brewing process even briefly (the spiral pour is the visual)
- Keep the talking-head delivery relaxed and conversational
- Send us the raw + the edit so we have options

Don't
- Don't use TikTok's trending sounds — we'll repost to our brand, and we want the audio to feel native to us
- Don't say "best pour-over" (we don't talk about ourselves that way)
- Don't make it look like a corporate ad (no zoom-ins on logo, no formal opening)
- Don't show competitor brands or any other coffee gear in frame
- Don't shoot under fluorescent light — kills the warmth we're going for

## Notes on this exemplar

- **Brief reads like a real human wrote it for a creator they respect** — not a corporate template
- **Hooks are voice-aligned to a calm/considered brand** — no exclamation points, no shouty CTAs
- **Each hook archetype is meaningfully different** (Problem-aware names a specific frustration; Contrarian challenges a category assumption — not minor word swaps)
- **B-roll shot list is shootable in 30 minutes** — all on a phone, all in one kitchen
- **Talking points are guidance, not script** — creators do this on their own voice
- **Don'ts include brand-voice things (no "best pour-over") plus practical things (no trending sounds)** — both matter
- **No FTC #ad required for organic** — buyer reposts on own channel
- **No usage rights paragraph for organic** — buyer owns the content via the work-for-hire arrangement
- The brief stays under 1.5 pages — long enough to be useful, short enough that creators actually read it
