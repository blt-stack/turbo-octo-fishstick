# Walmart Marketplace SEO

Walmart Marketplace's algorithm centers on the Listing Quality Score (LQS) — a 0-100 metric Walmart calculates per listing based on content completeness, attribute coverage, image quality, and reviews. High LQS = higher placement in search and category browse.

## Ranking factors (in rough order of impact)

1. **Listing Quality Score (LQS)** — composite metric Walmart shows in Seller Center. Driven by content completeness, attributes, image count and quality, ratings/reviews.
2. **Title keyword match**
3. **Attribute completeness** — same idea as eBay item specifics
4. **Bullet keyword coverage**
5. **Price competitiveness** — Walmart's algorithm prefers competitively-priced listings
6. **Description keyword coverage** (moderate)
7. **Seller performance metrics** — fulfillment speed, defect rate, returns (out of scope for copy)

## What this skill optimizes

### Title (Product Name) — hard cap 200 chars, target 50-75 chars
- Walmart penalizes keyword stuffing harder than Amazon does. Keep it natural.
- Formula: `[Brand] [Product Name] [Key Attribute] [Pack Size/Quantity]`
- No special characters, no symbols.
- Singular and concrete: "Stainless Steel Insulated Water Bottle 32 oz" beats "Premium Sleek Modern Stainless Steel Water Bottle for Hydration"
- 50-75 chars is the sweet spot — too short loses keywords, too long looks spammy.

### Key Features (Bullets) — 3-10 bullets, hard cap 80 chars each
- Yes, 80 chars — much shorter than Amazon. Concise wins.
- Front-load the benefit.
- Plain language — Walmart's audience is broader and more value-conscious than Amazon's.
- No marketing fluff ("amazing", "incredible", "perfect") — Walmart's algorithm flags it.
- Use all 10 bullet slots if you have substantive points; 3 bullets is the minimum for a high LQS.

### Description — 150-300 words sweet spot, no hard limit
- Plain prose. Walmart strips most formatting.
- Cover: what it is, who it's for, key features, what's in the box, materials, care.
- No CTAs, no external URLs (TOS).
- Mentioning competitors or other platforms is a TOS violation.

### Attributes — fill all category-specific attributes Walmart offers
- This is the highest-leverage LQS lever. Walmart categories typically offer 10-25 attribute fields.
- Required attributes are mandatory.
- Optional attributes are not required but heavily weighted by LQS.
- Each filled attribute pushes the LQS up; each blank one keeps it down.

## Bullet quality rubric (Walmart-specific)

Each bullet scored on:
- Opens with a benefit — 1 pt
- 60-80 chars utilization — 1 pt
- Plain language, no fluff words — 1 pt
- Distinct point not covered by other bullets — 1 pt

Sum across all bullets used (minimum 3, maximum 10), scale to the rubric's 20 pts.

## Completeness checks (Walmart-specific)

- Title 50-75 chars
- ≥3 bullets, ideally 6-10
- Description ≥150 words
- All required attributes filled
- At least 80% of optional attributes filled
- ≥4 product images (Walmart allows up to 12)
- Main image is on a white background (Walmart requirement)
- Product correctly categorized

## Common pitfalls

- **Title over 200 chars** — listing fails QA review.
- **Fluff words in bullets** — "best", "amazing", "perfect" trigger content-quality flags.
- **Skipping optional attributes** — directly drops LQS.
- **Mentioning competitors** — TOS violation.
- **Pricing language in copy** — Walmart controls price display.
- **Marketing claims without substantiation** — "FDA approved", "doctor recommended", etc. require verification.
