# Dispute Letter

The hardest letter to write. The shipment was wrong / defective / short / late. You need it resolved without burning the relationship if it's a long-term one you want to keep.

## Dispute categories

| Category | Common scenarios |
|---|---|
| Quality defects | Surface flaws, finish inconsistency, color drift, breakage on first use |
| Short shipment | Box count short of invoice, missing items, partial fulfillment |
| Wrong specs | Different material, wrong color, wrong dimensions, wrong packaging |
| Late shipment | Lead time blown, no proactive comms, missed launch window |
| Quality regression | Reorder is materially worse than first order |

The letter structure is similar across categories; the evidence requirements differ.

## Tone hierarchy

Disputes need calibrated firmness. Too soft and the supplier doesn't take it seriously. Too hard and the relationship is damaged.

The skill applies this tone gradient based on **severity × value × relationship-stage**:

- **Low severity (small finish issue) + established relationship**: very soft, almost casual mention
- **Medium severity (10% defect rate) + established relationship**: firm but warm
- **High severity (50%+ defects, missed launch) + established relationship**: serious tone, escalation path noted
- **Any severity + new relationship**: firmer, more documentation-forward
- **Any severity + strained relationship**: very formal, may include contract / payment-hold language

The skill flags severity classification in the .docx so the buyer can override.

## The dispute 5-part letter

### 1. Open

Regional opener — but more measured. Don't open warm if the dispute is severe.

### 2. Bridge — state the facts

This is the most important section. Specific, fact-based, no emotion:

> "Order #5118 arrived on March 12 (sea freight, 35 days transit). On inspection of the [N] units delivered, we found [specific issue] in [X%] of units."

Include:
- Order ID, ship date, arrival date
- Quantity affected (count + percentage)
- Specific defect description (use plain language, not legal language)
- Photos / video documentation (referenced; attached or linked)
- Comparison to spec or to first-order quality if a regression

### 3. Ask

Three common resolution structures:

**Refund / partial refund:**
> "We're seeking a credit on the affected units (X%, $Y) applied to the next order."

**Replacement:**
> "Could you reship the [N] affected units? We can absorb the customs and shipping costs to expedite, or have them included in the next production run."

**Inspection + plan:**
> "Could your quality team inspect the photos and confirm root cause? Once we understand whether this is a one-off or a production-batch issue, we can discuss the right resolution."

**Acknowledgment + dispute-window flag (when supplier may resist):**
> "Per our agreement, defective merchandise discovered within 30 days of arrival is eligible for resolution. We've documented the issue within that window and request your response."

### 4. Mutual benefit framing

For relationships worth saving:
> "This is the first quality issue we've seen across [N] orders together, and we're confident we can resolve it. We're committed to continuing our partnership and want to handle this in a way that preserves it."

For new relationships:
> "We want to understand whether this represents a typical outcome or an exception. The way we resolve it will determine whether we move forward together."

For strained relationships:
> No mutual-benefit framing — go straight to resolution ask.

### 5. Close

Specific timeline expectation:

> "Could you respond by [date]? Given the nature of this dispute, we'd appreciate a faster turnaround than usual."

## Length targets

Disputes are typically longer than other letters because they require documentation:
- China / Vietnam: 250-400 words
- India: 200-340 words
- North America / EU: 180-300 words

## Evidence is non-negotiable

Every dispute letter must include or reference:
1. Order number + date
2. Quantity affected + total quantity
3. Specific description of the issue
4. Photos / video documentation (attached in email or linked)
5. Date of inspection / discovery
6. Comparison to original spec, sample, or prior order if applicable

The skill prompts the buyer for these before drafting. If any are missing, the skill flags the gap rather than papering over it.

## What NEVER goes in a dispute letter

- Emotional language ("This is unacceptable," "I'm furious")
- Personal attacks on the contact person
- Hyperbole ("worst shipment we've ever received")
- Threats of contract / litigation in the first letter (escalate only if first letter doesn't resolve)
- Specific demands the supplier can't possibly meet (e.g., "next-day replacement from China" — impossible)
- Public threats (mentioning Trustpilot / public reviews) — burns the relationship; use only if walking away
- Vague language about quality ("not what we expected") — be specific or don't write

## Escalation path

The skill drafts the FIRST dispute letter. If the supplier's response is unsatisfactory, the next letters in the escalation:

1. **First dispute letter** (drafted here) — assumes good faith
2. **Second letter, 7-14 days later** if no resolution — same content, firmer tone, references first letter
3. **Escalation letter** — copies the supplier's sales manager / GM
4. **Payment-hold letter** — for ongoing orders, withholds balance pending resolution
5. **Walk-away letter** — last resort, final communication

The first letter does not threaten any of these — it assumes resolution will happen.

## Worked example — established China supplier, medium-severity quality issue

```
Subject: Order #5118 — quality issue on River Stone Pour-Over batch

Dear Ms. Chen,

I want to bring an issue to your attention from order #5118 (shipped February 12, arrived Brooklyn warehouse March 18).

Upon inspection of the 1500 units received, we identified glaze cracking in approximately 14% of units (210 of 1500). The cracking appears on the inner wall near the spout — same location across the affected units, suggesting a production-batch issue rather than handling damage.

Photos and detail shots are attached. For comparison, we've also included photos from order #5005 (October 2025) showing the standard glaze finish.

We'd like to propose:
1. Your QC team inspects the photos and confirms whether this is consistent with a production-batch issue
2. We discuss either: (a) credit on the 210 affected units to be applied to our next order, or (b) replacement of 210 units in our next production run, with the replacement units included at no charge

This is the first quality issue we've seen across four orders together, and we're confident we can resolve it. We're committed to continuing the partnership and want to handle this in a way that preserves it.

Could you respond by April 2? Given the timing of our next reorder (planned for late April), confirming the resolution before then would help us plan.

Best regards,
Jamie Carter
Stone & Glass
```

## Worked example — North America supplier, severe issue, new relationship

```
Subject: Order #4421 — shipment short + spec mismatch (urgent)

Hi Mike,

Order #4421 (received March 14) has two issues:

1. Short shipment: invoice shows 800 satchels; we received 720. Missing 80 units.
2. Spec mismatch: 320 units shipped with the wrong stitching pattern (cross-stitch instead of saddle-stitch). Per the PO and approved sample, all units should have been saddle-stitch.

Photos of received vs. spec comparison attached. Box count documented via warehouse intake (also attached).

Resolution needed:
- 80 units short-shipped: ship the missing units expedited (FedEx 2-day at your cost) OR credit invoice for 80 units
- 320 units wrong stitching: we can hold + sell at discount, or return them — please advise
- Need response by Friday, March 20, given launch timing

I'd like to get this resolved cleanly. Let's get on a call this week.

Jamie
Stone & Glass
```

Notice the NA dispute letter:
- Numbered facts upfront
- No relationship preamble
- Specific timeline
- Phone-call offer
- Two resolution paths offered
- Firm but not hostile
