# Payment Terms Letter

Asks to move from 100% upfront → deposit + balance, from TT to LC, from full deposit to escrow, or from upfront to NET 30 / NET 60.

## Why payment terms exist

Suppliers default to favorable-to-them terms because:
- **Working capital** — they fund raw materials and labor before the order ships
- **Default risk** — overseas customers, no contracts enforceable cross-border, no easy recourse
- **Cash flow** — most factories run on tight cash; deposits fund the next order
- **Relationship signal** — new buyers get the strictest terms; established buyers earn flexibility

Movement is possible but follows a typical progression:

```
First order:            100% upfront  OR  30% deposit + 70% before shipping
Second-third order:     30% deposit + 70% on B/L copy (TT)
Fourth+ order:          30% deposit + 70% NET 30  OR  0% deposit + NET 30
Long-established:       NET 30, NET 45, NET 60, or rolling credit
```

You can't skip stages. Asking for NET 30 on the first order from a new supplier reads as inexperienced.

## The payment terms 5-part letter

### 1. Open

Regional opener.

### 2. Bridge

Reference order history. The ask is justified by the relationship:

> "We've now completed four orders together (totaling $X in volume), all paid on time per our 30/70 TT terms."

### 3. Ask

**Move from 100% upfront → 30/70:**
> "Now that we've completed our first two orders, would you be open to moving from 100% upfront to a 30/70 deposit/balance structure for upcoming orders?"

**Move from 30/70 TT (before shipping) → 30/70 TT against B/L copy:**
> "Could we structure the 70% balance against B/L copy rather than before shipping? This is standard for our relationships at this volume and helps our cash position."

**Move from TT → LC:**
> "For larger orders going forward, we'd like to move to LC at sight. Our bank is [name]; I can have them send the LC draft this week."

**Move to NET terms:**
> "We'd like to discuss moving to NET 30 for orders going forward. We've now completed [N] orders with on-time payment; we'd be comfortable with a 30% deposit + 70% NET 30 structure to start."

### 4. Mutual benefit framing

The supplier needs reassurance:
> "We understand this is a meaningful change. Our payment record speaks to the relationship we want to maintain, and we're happy to structure the transition gradually — perhaps NET 30 on the next order with a return to deposit if needed."

### 5. Close

> "Could you share your view by [date]? If your accounting team needs additional documentation from our side (bank reference, trade history, etc.), we're happy to provide."

## Length targets

Same as MOQ letter.

## What NEVER goes in a payment terms letter

- Asking for NET 30 / NET 60 on the first order (reads as inexperienced)
- Implication that the supplier's terms are "unreasonable" or "too strict"
- "We're tight on cash this month" — never reveal cash pressure
- Asking for terms loosening immediately after a quality dispute (terrible timing)
- Asking via Alibaba TradeMessenger for major terms changes — use direct email for serious term shifts

## Order of operations

Payment terms are typically the THIRD ask, not the first:

1. First: get pricing right
2. Second: get quality right (samples, first order quality)
3. Third: get payment terms right (after you've proven yourself)

A buyer asking for NET 30 in the first email to a new supplier is asking the wrong question first.

## Worked example — established China supplier, moving from TT-before-shipping to TT-against-B/L

```
Subject: Payment terms request for upcoming orders

Dear Ms. Chen,

Hope you and your family are well after Lunar New Year.

We've now completed four orders together (totaling $48,000 over the past 14 months), all paid promptly per our 30/70 TT terms. Thank you for the consistent quality and on-time shipping — it's been an excellent relationship.

As we plan larger orders for 2026, we'd like to discuss moving the 70% balance from "before shipping" to "against B/L copy." This is standard for our relationships at this volume and helps us manage our cash position around growing inventory.

We understand this is a meaningful change. We're happy to discuss any documentation that would be helpful on your side — bank reference, references from other US-based brands we work with, or anything else.

Could you let us know your view by April 30? Happy to discuss on a call if easier.

Best regards,
Jamie Carter
Stone & Glass
```

## Worked example — North America supplier, moving from NET 30 to NET 45

```
Subject: Payment terms — proposal for NET 45

Hi Mike,

Quick ask. We're at the volume now where moving from NET 30 to NET 45 would help us better align with our retail-payment cycle (NET 30 from our wholesale customers means cash arrives just as your invoice is due).

Track record: 8 orders, all paid on or before due date. Open to any reference or documentation you'd want.

If NET 45 isn't a fit, no worries — happy to stay at NET 30. Let me know your thinking.

Thanks,
Jamie
```

Notice the NA letter:
- Specific operational reason (matches retail payment cycle)
- Cites track record briefly
- Offers an easy out
- Shorter than the China version because the relationship doesn't need rebuilding in every email
