# Region: EU

European suppliers operate in 24+ languages, multiple regulatory regimes, and a generally more formal business communication style than NA. The skill applies a "formal but practical" register and flags regulatory considerations when relevant (CE marking, REACH compliance, GDPR for B2B data).

## Opening conventions

### Salutations

- **"Dear Mr. [Lastname]"** or **"Dear Ms. [Lastname]"** — default for first contact
- **"Dear [Firstname] [Lastname]"** — used in some EU countries (Germany, Netherlands often)
- **"Dear [Firstname]"** — once the relationship is established
- **"Hi [Firstname]"** — after substantial relationship; less universal in EU than NA

### Country-specific conventions

| Country | Notes |
|---|---|
| Germany | More formal even after established relationship; "Sehr geehrter Herr/Frau" in German equivalent; titles (Dr., Prof.) used carefully |
| Netherlands | More direct than Germany; first names earlier; English near-universal |
| France | Formal salutations preferred; "Monsieur/Madame [Lastname]" in French equivalent; English fluent but slightly less direct than NL |
| Italy | Warm + formal; some relationship preamble appreciated |
| Spain / Portugal | Warm + slightly relationship-oriented; "Estimado/a" in Spanish equivalent |
| Poland / Czech Republic | Formal first contact; switches faster to direct once relationship established |
| UK (post-Brexit but culturally still EU-style) | British politeness conventions — slightly more indirect than NA, more formal than NL |
| Scandinavia (Sweden, Denmark, Norway, Finland) | Direct, brief, English near-universal; least formal of EU |

The skill prompts buyer for country if EU is selected.

### Opening line

1. **"Dear Mr. Müller, I hope this message finds you well."** — formal, common
2. **"Dear Ms. Rossi, I hope you are well."** — slightly less formal
3. **"Dear Andreas, I trust all is well at the factory."** — established relationship, slightly more warm
4. **"Hi Erik, hope you're well."** — Scandinavian / Dutch direct

## Pacing of asks

EU letters tend to be more structured than NA — clear paragraphs, organized progression of points. Less casual than NA, less elaborate than Chinese.

The 5-part letter applies; structure is often slightly more explicit (paragraphs more clearly demarcated).

## Numeric specificity

Very specific, similar to NA:

- "We require 500 units by 30 April."
- "Could you quote €3.80 per unit at 2,000 units?"
- Date format: typically DD/MM/YYYY in EU (not MM/DD/YYYY as in US) — match supplier's convention

## Pacing expectations

Reply windows in EU:
- 48 hours: normal pace
- 48-96 hours: acceptable, especially in August/late December
- 96+ hours: nudge

EU business hours: 9 AM - 5 PM local time, M-F. Lunch break tradition (often noon-1pm, longer in southern Europe).

## Holidays — significant impact

The skill checks against these widely-observed EU holidays:

| Holiday | Approximate dates | Impact |
|---|---|---|
| New Year | Jan 1 | 1-2 days |
| Carnival (DE, NL, parts of FR/IT/ES) | Feb (varies) | 1-3 days; partial week in Catholic regions |
| Good Friday + Easter Monday | March/April (varies) | 1-4 days widely observed |
| Labour Day (most EU) | May 1 | 1 day |
| Ascension Day + Whit Monday | May/June (varies) | 1-2 days widely observed in much of EU |
| Assumption of Mary | August 15 | Major in Catholic countries (IT, ES, PT, FR partially) |
| **August summer holidays** | Aug 1 - 31 | **Major slowdown** — many factories close 2-3 weeks; Southern Europe especially |
| All Saints' Day | November 1 | 1 day (Catholic countries) |
| Christmas / New Year | Dec 23 - Jan 6 | Major slowdown — most businesses largely closed |

August is the major EU-wide slowdown. Equivalent to Chinese Lunar New Year for supply chain impact. The skill flags August letters with a warning.

## Honorifics in body

- "Sir" / "Madam" used moderately in formal letters; less in established correspondence
- Academic titles (Dr., Prof.) used carefully in Germany, Austria, parts of Eastern Europe
- "Du" vs. "Sie" (German), "tu" vs. "Lei" (Italian) distinction — when in German/Italian, default to formal until invited

## Regulatory considerations

EU suppliers often expect references to regulatory compliance:

- **CE marking** — for products requiring conformity (electronics, toys, machinery)
- **REACH** — chemical compliance, relevant for plastics, textiles, finishes
- **CPSIA equivalent in EU** — children's products
- **GDPR** — data protection, relevant if exchanging customer data
- **EU labelling regulations** — language requirements for product packaging
- **Country of origin** — required for EU import

The skill flags these in letters where the product type triggers them. New suppliers in EU appreciate this signal of buyer sophistication.

## Mutual benefit framing

EU suppliers respond well to:

- Long-term reliability framing ("stable annual volume," "multi-year commitment")
- Quality framing ("our brand stands for quality, which is why your factory's reputation drew us")
- Documentation framing ("we'd like to formalize the relationship with proper contracts and SLA")

Less effective: aggressive cost-cutting framing, which EU suppliers may interpret as commodity-buyer signal.

## Sign-off

- **"Kind regards, [Firstname Lastname]"** — most common
- **"Best regards, [Firstname Lastname]"** — also common
- **"With kind regards, [Firstname Lastname]"** — formal, common in DE/NL
- **"Sincerely, [Firstname Lastname]"** — formal, fine for new contacts
- **"Yours faithfully"** — old-fashioned; only if you opened with "Dear Sir/Madam"
- **"Yours sincerely"** — old-fashioned; only if you opened with "Dear Mr/Ms"

Avoid: "Cheers" (works in UK casual but not most of EU), "Talk soon."

## Length targets in EU region

- New supplier first contact: 180-280 words
- Established supplier routine ask: 140-220 words
- Dispute letter: 240-380 words
- Quick follow-up after silence: 60-120 words

## Phrases that work specifically well in EU

- "We appreciate your detailed reply."
- "We look forward to formalizing our cooperation."
- "Please find the relevant documentation attached."
- "Our planning relies on the timely receipt of..."
- "We trust this works on your side."

## What to avoid in EU

- Overly casual openers (especially DE, FR, IT)
- US-style superlatives ("amazing," "awesome," "best ever") — read as unprofessional
- Aggressive cost-cutting language without context — read as low-quality buyer
- Sending an urgent ask in August (the supplier may legitimately be on holiday for 2-3 weeks)

## Bilingual considerations

Most EU suppliers serving English-speaking brands communicate in English. However:

- French, German, Italian factories sometimes prefer first communication in the native language
- The skill defaults to English; flags if buyer wants native-language version drafted (would require buyer's language fluency or translation)
- When supplier writes in their native language and English, mirror back — don't try to switch them to English-only
