# Sample Request Letter

Get samples before committing to a production run. Samples are the most reliable way to evaluate a new supplier without burning $5K+ on a first-order quality bet.

## The three common sample structures

| Structure | When it works | When to ask |
|---|---|---|
| **Free samples (supplier pays everything)** | Established suppliers; supplier has stock samples ready | First or second outreach to suppliers with catalog SKUs |
| **Paid samples (buyer pays supplier + shipping)** | Custom samples, multiple variations, new tooling | Default for serious new-supplier evaluations |
| **Sample credit against first order** | Custom samples worth $50-300; supplier wants commitment signal | When you're 70% likely to place an order; the sample fee gets credited back |

Pick ONE structure based on the supplier's likely position. Asking for free samples from a brand-new supplier on a custom product reads as inexperienced.

## The sample request 5-part letter

### 1. Open

Regional opener. For very new suppliers (first contact ever), the opener may include who you are and how you found them.

### 2. Bridge

Reference the specific product / catalog reference:

> "We're interested in your [product / catalog SKU]. We saw the listing on Alibaba / your website / via [referrer] and would like to evaluate the product in person before discussing larger orders."

### 3. Ask

**Paid samples ask (most common, professional):**
> "Could you send 2-3 samples? We're happy to pay the sample cost plus expedited shipping. Could you quote the sample cost and DHL/FedEx shipping to [city, country]?"

**Sample-credit-against-first-order ask:**
> "Could you send 2-3 samples at your sample cost plus shipping? If we move to a production order within 60 days, we'd appreciate the sample cost being credited toward the first order. Standard practice for evaluations on our side."

**Free samples ask (only when supplier has stock samples and the request is small):**
> "If you have a stock sample available, we'd be grateful if you could send one. We'd cover shipping via DHL/FedEx to [address]."

### 4. Mutual benefit framing

Tell the supplier why they're investing in this:

> "We're a [brand description, growing / established] focused on [product category] for the [US / EU / global] market. If the sample meets our spec, we'd plan an initial order of [N] units in [timeframe]."

This is the buyer-quality signal. Without it, the supplier doesn't know if you're a tire-kicker or a serious buyer.

### 5. Close

> "Could you confirm sample availability + cost by [date]? Once confirmed, I'll send DHL account details and the shipping address."

## Length targets

Sample requests can be shorter than other letters since the ask is concrete:
- China / Vietnam: 140-220 words
- India: 100-180 words
- North America / EU: 80-140 words

## Sample request specifics — what to ask for

The skill prompts the buyer to specify:

1. **Number of samples** — typically 2-5; one to inspect, one to test/destruct, one to keep
2. **Variations** — color options, size options, materials, finishes
3. **Quantity per variation** — usually 1 each unless durability test needed
4. **Specific specs to confirm** — material grams, dimensions ±tolerance, packaging
5. **Shipping speed** — expedited (DHL/FedEx) is standard for samples; never sea freight
6. **Shipping account** — buyer's DHL/FedEx account vs. supplier's
7. **Customs / duties** — buyer almost always pays; flag this explicitly

## What NEVER goes in a sample letter

- Asking for "a few samples" without a number — ambiguous, invites slow response
- Asking for samples without committing to evaluate them — supplier wastes effort if you ghost
- Implying the samples will determine if you "consider" the supplier — sounds entitled
- Requesting customization on the sample (custom packaging, custom logo) when the supplier hasn't yet confirmed they'll work with you — wait until the relationship warms

## Sample evaluation back-promise

A good sample letter includes a soft commitment to give feedback:

> "Once we receive the samples (we'll inspect within 5 business days of arrival), I'll send you a detailed evaluation with our notes and next-step thinking."

Suppliers appreciate this; many sample requests vanish into silence after the samples arrive, which trains suppliers to be more cautious on future sample requests.

## Worked example — new China supplier, custom sample with sample-credit ask

```
Subject: Sample request — River Stone Pour-Over evaluation

Dear Ms. Chen,

We're Stone & Glass, a US-based ceramics brand. We discovered your factory via Alibaba and are evaluating the River Stone Pour-Over (your catalog reference RS-2400) for our 2026 lineup.

Before discussing a production order, we'd like to evaluate samples in person. Could you send three samples:
- 2 in the standard glaze (Stone)
- 1 in the alternate glaze (Slate)

We're happy to pay sample cost plus DHL Express shipping to our office in Brooklyn (zip 11201). Could you quote the sample cost and shipping?

We'd also propose: if we proceed to a production order within 60 days of receiving the samples, the sample cost is credited toward the first order. This is standard for our evaluations.

Once we receive the samples, we'll inspect within 5 business days and send a detailed evaluation back to you with notes on weight, finish, and packaging.

If the samples meet our spec, we'd plan an initial order of 300-500 units in the next quarter.

Could you confirm sample availability and quote the cost by April 5?

Best regards,
Jamie Carter
Stone & Glass
```

## Worked example — established NA supplier, quick sample ask

```
Subject: Sample of the new satchel pattern — pls send 2

Hi Mike,

Quick one. Can you send 2 samples of the satchel in the brown leather we discussed? I have your DHL account on file; we'll pay sample cost + shipping per usual.

Will inspect and send notes by end of next week.

Thanks,
Jamie
```

Notice: no relationship preamble, no future-volume framing, no commit-back promise. The relationship history covers all that.
