# Region: India

Indian suppliers are typically English-fluent and operate at a faster business pace than China. Communication is more direct, but formality varies — first contact warrants more formality than ongoing.

## Opening conventions

### Honorifics

- **"Dear Mr. [Lastname]"** or **"Dear Ms. [Lastname]"** — standard for first contact
- **"Dear [Firstname]"** — common after first reply
- **"Hi [Firstname]"** — once the relationship is established

Common surnames: Sharma, Singh, Patel, Kumar, Shah, Mehta, Gupta, Reddy, Iyer, Khan. Note: South Indian names often have surnames as initials ("S. Kumar" — the S is the father's name); Northern names follow Western order.

### Opening line

Acceptable openers:

1. **"Dear Mr. Sharma, hope you are doing well."** — standard
2. **"Dear Mr. Sharma, hope this email finds you well."** — slightly more formal
3. **"Dear Sharma ji, hope all is well at your end."** — warmer; "ji" is a respectful suffix
4. **"Hi Rohit, hope you had a great Diwali."** — established relationship, around the holiday

## Pacing of asks

Indian suppliers can handle a more direct structure than Chinese ones. The 5-part letter still applies, but the bridge can be shorter:

- China bridge: 3-4 sentences
- India bridge: 2-3 sentences

Asks can be slightly more numerically specific without softening.

## Numeric specificity

Indians are comfortable with specific numbers and direct asks:

- "We'd like 250 units at $4.20 each."
- "Could you commit to 30-day production lead time?"
- "What's your best price at 1500 units?"

You can be more direct here than in China without losing the relationship.

## Pacing expectations

Reply windows in India:
- Same business day: high-engagement supplier
- 24-48 hours: normal pace
- 48-72 hours: starting to slow; soft follow-up
- 72+ hours: assume something needs nudging

India business hours: 9 AM - 6 PM IST (UTC+5:30), Monday-Saturday. Sunday is off for most.

## Holidays — significant impact

| Holiday | Approximate dates | Impact |
|---|---|---|
| Republic Day | January 26 | 1 day |
| Holi | March (varies; 1-2 days) | Minor |
| Eid al-Fitr | Varies (~late March / early April) | 1-2 days |
| Independence Day | August 15 | 1 day |
| Gandhi Jayanti | October 2 | 1 day |
| Diwali | October or November (5 days) | Major — factories largely shut for 3-7 days |
| Christmas / New Year | Late December | Minor for most; significant for Christian-majority regions like Kerala, Goa |

Diwali is the major shutdown period — equivalent to Lunar New Year in China but shorter. Avoid sending dispute letters in the Diwali run-up week.

## Honorifics in body

- "Sir" and "Ma'am" are commonly used as politeness signals — even in established relationships
- Don't overdo "Sir/Ma'am" — once per email is plenty
- "Yourself" instead of "you" is a regional English variant — common, not wrong
- Slightly more formal vocabulary than American English

## Common Indian English variations

These are correct English, just regionally distinct. Don't try to "correct" them in replies — match the supplier's register:

- "Kindly do the needful" — equivalent to "please handle this"
- "Please revert" — please reply
- "Please update the same" — please update this
- "Same to be done" — to be completed
- "PFB" / "PFA" — "please find below" / "please find attached"

When drafting, use standard professional English — don't try to imitate Indian English unless the supplier writes in that register first.

## Mutual benefit framing

Indians respond well to clear long-term framing:

- "We see this as a multi-year relationship."
- "If our forecast holds, we'd be ordering quarterly through 2027."
- "We're committed to building stable volume with the right partner."

## Sign-off

- **"Best regards"** — most common
- **"Warm regards"** — slightly warmer, also common
- **"Kind regards"** — formal
- **"Thanks and regards"** — common in India specifically
- **"Sincerely"** — formal, fine for new contacts

## Length targets in India region

- New supplier first contact: 180-260 words
- Established supplier routine ask: 140-220 words
- Dispute letter: 240-360 words
- Quick follow-up after silence: 60-120 words

## Direct contact preferences

Most Indian suppliers prefer:
1. Email (most professional)
2. WhatsApp Business (very common for quick follow-ups; share number once relationship is established)
3. Phone calls (Indian business culture rewards phone follow-ups more than China; suggest calls for complex disputes)
4. Alibaba/IndiaMART TradeMessenger (entry point, but move to email quickly)

## Phrases that work specifically well in India

- "We appreciate the prompt response."
- "Looking forward to a long-term partnership."
- "Kindly let us know your thoughts."
- "Please do the needful at your end."
- "Hope to work together for many years."

## Phrases that DON'T translate well

- "Touch base" — slightly confusing
- "Circle back" — same
- "Bandwidth" — uncommon, may confuse
- "Loop me in" — confusing
- American slang in general

## Cultural nuance — bargaining is expected

Indian business culture is more openly negotiation-friendly than Chinese. Saying "your price seems high; can we discuss?" is not face-losing in India the way it would be in China. The skill softens slightly for India but doesn't go to full indirect-framing mode.
