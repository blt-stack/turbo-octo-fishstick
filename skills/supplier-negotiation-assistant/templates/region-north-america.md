# Region: North America (US / Canada / Mexico)

The most direct communication style of any region. Lead with the ask, justify with brevity, expect fast replies. Mexico has some bilingual considerations but most US-facing Mexican suppliers communicate in English by default.

## Opening conventions

### Salutations

- **"Hi [Firstname]"** — most common; default for established contacts
- **"Hi [Firstname], hope you're well"** — warmer
- **"Dear Mr./Ms. [Lastname]"** — only for first contact, formal industries (legal, regulated goods), or very large suppliers
- **"Hey [Firstname]"** — only after substantial relationship

US/Canadian/Mexican business culture has lost much of the formal letter-writing style; emails tend to read more like memos.

### Opening line

Most letters skip a fluffy opener entirely:

- "Hi Mike, quick question about the next order..."
- "Hi Sarah, following up on the quote you sent last week..."
- "Hi Carlos, hope all's well — wanted to ask about..."

Acceptable; warmer than Asian conventions because the relational signal is implicit.

## Pacing of asks

**Lead with the ask. Justify after.** The 5-part letter still applies but compressed:

- Open: 1 sentence (often combined with bridge)
- Bridge: 1-2 sentences
- Ask: 1-3 sentences (specific)
- Mutual benefit: 1 sentence (often omitted entirely)
- Close: 1-2 sentences

The whole letter often fits in 5-8 sentences.

## Numeric specificity

Extremely specific. NA suppliers expect numbers:

- "We need 500 units by April 30."
- "Can you do $3.80/unit at 2000?"
- "NET 45 instead of NET 30 — same volume."

Vagueness reads as unprofessional.

## Pacing expectations

Reply windows in NA:
- Same business day: high-engagement
- 24 hours: normal pace
- 48 hours: starting to slow
- 72 hours: nudge

US business hours: 9 AM - 5 PM in your supplier's time zone, M-F.
Canada: same.
Mexico: 9 AM - 6 PM, M-F (lunch break tradition runs longer; afternoon emails often replied next morning).

## Holidays

| Holiday | Date | Impact |
|---|---|---|
| New Year's Day | Jan 1 | 1 day |
| MLK Day (US) | 3rd Monday Jan | 1 day |
| Presidents' Day (US) | 3rd Monday Feb | 1 day |
| Good Friday | Varies | 1 day (CA more than US) |
| Memorial Day (US) | Last Monday May | 1 day + long weekend |
| Canada Day | Jul 1 | 1 day |
| US Independence Day | Jul 4 | 1 day |
| Labor Day | 1st Monday Sep | 1 day + long weekend |
| Thanksgiving (US) | 4th Thursday Nov | 1-2 days; week is often slow |
| Thanksgiving (CA) | 2nd Monday Oct | 1 day |
| Christmas / New Year | Dec 23 - Jan 1 | Major slowdown — many businesses close Dec 23-Jan 2 |

Mexico:
- Constitution Day (Feb 5), Benito Juárez (Mar 21), Labor Day (May 1), Independence Day (Sep 16), Día de la Revolución (Nov 20), Christmas/New Year period
- The week between Christmas and New Year is widely observed

The skill warns the buyer of holiday timing where it overlaps with the letter's deadline.

## Honorifics in body

- "Mr." / "Ms." rare after first email
- "Sir" / "Ma'am" — almost never used; reads as old-fashioned or sarcastic in modern NA correspondence
- First name basis quickly

## Mutual benefit framing

Often omitted entirely. When included, very brief:

- "Good for both sides given the volume."
- "Win-win."
- "Aligned with what you mentioned last call."

Long mutual-benefit paragraphs read as padding to NA readers.

## Phone-call offers

Common in NA — many supplier negotiations resolve faster via a 15-minute call than 5 email exchanges. The skill includes a call offer in letters where:

- The ask is complex (combined ask, dispute, payment terms change)
- The relationship is established (calls feel natural at this stage)
- The buyer has indicated phone availability

Standard phrasing:
> "Happy to jump on a quick call if it's easier — I'm free [day] morning or [day] afternoon."

## Sign-off

- **"Thanks"** — most common, casual-professional
- **"Thanks, [Firstname]"** — same
- **"Best, [Firstname]"** — slightly warmer
- **"Cheers, [Firstname]"** — common, slightly casual
- **"Best regards, [Firstname Lastname]"** — slightly formal
- **"Sincerely"** — too formal for NA in most cases

## Length targets in NA region

- New supplier first contact: 120-200 words
- Established supplier routine ask: 60-140 words
- Dispute letter: 180-300 words (longer for documentation)
- Quick follow-up after silence: 30-80 words

NA letters are about half the length of equivalent China letters.

## Mexico-specific considerations

Most Mexican manufacturers serving US/Canada brands communicate in English. Some considerations:

- Bilingual (English-Spanish) is acceptable; lead in English unless supplier writes in Spanish first
- "Buenos días" / "Saludos" as Spanish opener if the supplier has used Spanish — otherwise English
- Mexican business culture is slightly more relationship-warm than US/Canadian; soften slightly
- Major manufacturers in Tijuana, Monterrey, Guadalajara — proximity to US makes faster shipping a competitive advantage to emphasize

## Phrases that work specifically well in NA

- "Quick one for you..."
- "Just want to make sure we're aligned..."
- "Want to confirm by [day]..."
- "Let me know what works."
- "Appreciate the quick turnaround."

## What to avoid

- Long flowery openers ("I hope this email finds you well during this beautiful spring season...")
- Excessive formality (NA reads "Dear Mr. Smith" as out-of-touch after initial contact)
- Burying the ask after paragraphs of preamble (NA readers will scan past)
- "Friendly" reminder ("Just a friendly reminder...") — passive-aggressive register in NA business culture
