# Platform: LinkedIn

LinkedIn creator outreach is the most B2B-adjacent of any platform. Creators here are often working professionals, consultants, executives, or thought leaders. Most relevant for brands in B2B, professional services, productivity, wellness, or premium consumer goods that overlap with professional life.

## Channel options

- **LinkedIn Message** (free): primary, but limited if not connected
- **InMail** (paid via LinkedIn Premium / Sales Navigator): premium; reads as more serious
- **Connection request with personalized note**: hybrid — sends a 300-character message via connect
- **Email** (if listed in profile): preferred for serious business pitches

The skill defaults to:
- Nano/micro: free LinkedIn message
- Mid-tier/macro: InMail (more reliable open rate)
- Plus connection request with note as a complementary follow-up

## Length

LinkedIn supports longer messages than IG/TikTok DMs:

| Tier | Message length |
|---|---|
| Nano | 120-200 words |
| Micro | 150-280 words |
| Mid-tier | 200-350 words |
| Macro | 280-400 words (often InMail) |
| Email (any tier) | 250-400 words |

LinkedIn readers expect more business context than other platforms. A short LinkedIn message reads as low-effort.

## Tone

- **Nano/Micro**: professional warm; "Hi [Name]" greeting standard
- **Mid-tier/Macro**: business correspondence; "Hi [Title] [Name]" if applicable
- Always use the creator's full first name; LinkedIn culture values name use

## Connection request notes

LinkedIn's connection request notes are limited to 300 characters (~50-60 words). They're the most-skimmed outreach format on any platform.

Structure:
```
Hi [First name] — your recent post on [topic] resonated. I run [brand], a [category] brand for [audience]. Would love to discuss a partnership idea if open to it. Best, [name]
```

Keep it under 300 characters. Be specific about the post that resonated.

## LinkedIn content format reality

LinkedIn creators primarily produce:
- Long-form posts (text + images)
- Newsletter (LinkedIn's native newsletter feature)
- Video posts
- Document carousels (PDF slides)
- Live events

Brand partnerships on LinkedIn typically take the form of:
- Sponsored post (creator writes a post mentioning brand)
- Newsletter sponsorship
- LinkedIn Live event sponsorship
- Co-created content (interview, case study)

The skill recommends partnership types based on the creator's primary content format (from pasted post context).

## Personalization on LinkedIn

LinkedIn personalization expects awareness of the creator's professional context:

> "Hi Priya,
>
> Your post last week about hiring patterns in early-stage startups specifically called out the 'culture-fit' anti-pattern — a framing I've been wrestling with as we hire our second team member. I run [brand], a [category] brand, and we'd love to collaborate on a piece about [related topic]."

Specific post reference + professional alignment + business-context brand intro.

## Required disclosures on LinkedIn

- **Gifted only**: "Thanks to [brand] for sending [product]" — FTC requires
- **Hybrid / Paid**: #ad #sponsored in the post body; LinkedIn doesn't have a built-in paid partnership tag yet
- **Affiliate**: "I receive commission" disclosure

LinkedIn disclosure enforcement is lighter than Instagram but FTC rules apply equally.

## Outreach structure (LinkedIn-specific)

```
Hi [First name],

[Personalized opener referencing specific post / professional context — 2 sentences]

[Brand intro with business context — 2-3 sentences including what the brand does and who it's for]

[Why this partnership would fit their professional content — 2 sentences]

[Specific offer — content type, structure, payment — 3-4 sentences]

[Clear ask + suggested timing — 1-2 sentences]

[Sign-off + role + brand name]
```

## Common LinkedIn outreach mistakes

- **Generic "I'd love to connect" message with no context**: ignored
- **Hard sell in connection request**: LinkedIn culture punishes this
- **Treating LinkedIn creators like consumer-platform creators**: wrong register
- **Asking for "thought leadership" content without specificity**: vague = no response
- **Sending obvious mass-templated InMails**: LinkedIn's algorithm detects and reduces InMail credit ratings
- **Pitching without acknowledging the creator's existing professional content**: looks like lazy targeting

## LinkedIn-specific tactical recommendations

- **InMail credit is rate-limited** (5-50/month depending on Premium tier); spend them on highest-value prospects
- **LinkedIn message read receipts work for connected contacts**: useful follow-up signal
- **Send messages Tuesday-Thursday, 10 AM-3 PM creator's time zone**: business hours matter on LinkedIn
- **Avoid Monday morning (catching up) and Friday afternoon (winding down)**
- **Reference specific company affiliations in the brand intro**: LinkedIn culture values institutional anchoring
- **Provide a clear business reason for the partnership**: LinkedIn audience needs to understand why this benefits them professionally
- **Use the "Add a note" feature on connection requests**: connection requests without notes are routinely ignored

## When LinkedIn outreach fits

LinkedIn outreach makes most sense for:
- Professional/office productivity products
- Wellness brands targeting working professionals
- Premium goods that overlap with corporate gifting
- B2B services pitched to professional creators
- Brands that have a credible business angle, not just a consumer one

For purely consumer brands (skincare, food, fashion), Instagram/TikTok are usually higher-ROI. The skill flags this when the brand category doesn't naturally fit LinkedIn's professional context.
