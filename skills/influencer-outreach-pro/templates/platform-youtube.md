# Platform: YouTube

YouTube creators operate differently from short-form creators. Higher production value per video, longer creative cycles, often-managed business operations. Outreach is typically email-first, not DM-first.

## Channel options

- **Email via business email in About tab**: primary channel
- **YouTube Channel DM** (if enabled): limited; many creators turn off
- **Email via manager** (if listed): preferred for mid-tier+
- **Comment** (warm replies only)

The skill defaults to email-first for YouTube; falls back to DM if no email found.

## Length

YouTube outreach is typically email — longer than DMs:

| Tier | Email length |
|---|---|
| Nano | 180-280 words |
| Micro | 220-350 words |
| Mid-tier | 280-400 words |
| Macro | 350-500 words (often to manager) |

Email format includes a subject line, proper greeting, body, and signature.

## Tone

- **Nano**: warm professional ("Hi [Name]," — full punctuation)
- **Micro**: professional
- **Mid-tier**: business-aware, references their channel as a business
- **Macro**: formal; assume manager-screening

## Subject line patterns

Subject lines are critical for email. Good patterns:

- "Partnership idea for [channel name]"
- "[Brand name] x [channel name] — collaboration"
- "Collab question for [first name]"
- "[Specific product reference] — partnership for the next video?"

Bad subject lines:
- "PARTNERSHIP OPPORTUNITY!!"
- "Sponsored content from [Brand]"
- "Quick question" (too vague; flagged as spam)
- "Re: " (don't fake a reply chain)

## YouTube content production reality

YouTube videos take 8-40 hours to produce. Creators don't make impulsive decisions about what to feature.

This affects outreach:
- **Lead time matters**: ask for partnerships 4-12 weeks before the brand's preferred posting date
- **One creator can produce ~1-4 videos per month**: don't expect quick turnaround
- **Concept-fit matters more than discount**: YouTube creators need to see the product fitting their video format, not just the price

## Specific personalization on YouTube

Reference specific recent videos by title or topic:

> "Hi Sarah,
>
> I just watched your video on minimal kitchen setups (the one where you tested the 5-piece challenge) — your point about handle ergonomics on knife sets really resonated."

Note: specific video reference, specific moment, specific takeaway. This proves the outreach is real.

## Required disclosures on YouTube

- **Gifted only**: verbal disclosure in video + "I received this product from [brand]" in description + #gift
- **Hybrid / Paid**: YouTube's "Paid promotion" toggle MUST be on (legally required); #ad + #sponsored + verbal disclosure in first 30 seconds
- **Affiliate**: "This video contains affiliate links" in description + verbal mention if applicable

YouTube's monetization rules + FTC overlap heavily; the skill embeds these expectations in outreach.

## Outreach structure (YouTube email-specific)

```
Subject: [Specific subject — references their channel or recent video]

Hi [First name],

[Personalized opener referencing a specific recent video — 1-2 sentences]

[Brand intro — 2-3 sentences including audience + product line]

[Product fit — 2-3 sentences on why this product would fit their channel format]

[Offer + structure — 2-4 sentences on what's being offered, expected deliverables, payment, timeline]

[Clear ask — 1-2 sentences on what response is needed]

[Easy out — "if it's not a fit, no worries"]

[Sign-off + brand contact info]
```

## Common YouTube outreach mistakes

- **Asking creators to "make a video" without specifying length / format / placement**: ambiguous deliverables = no response
- **Offering free product with vague "we'd love your honest review"**: doesn't compensate for production time; creators see through this
- **Pitching SKUs that don't fit the channel format**: a 10-min cooking tutorial channel won't make a 1-min product reveal
- **Sending to a "youtube@" address**: usually unread; find the personal/business email in About tab
- **No subject line that hooks**: gets buried in inbox
- **Pitching during creator's known break / hiatus**: if they posted "taking a break" recently, wait

## YouTube-specific tactical recommendations

- **Send emails Tuesday-Thursday, 10 AM - 2 PM creator's time zone**: highest open rate
- **Allow 7-14 days for first reply** (longer than IG/TikTok)
- **Be specific about whether you want a dedicated video or integration**: very different ask
- **Pre-set a video lead time**: "we're flexible on timing, ideally publish by [date]"
- **For mid-tier+, expect a media kit + rate card response**: don't be put off; this is normal business
- **Offer to send product first**: standard for YouTube; creators test before committing

## Integration vs. dedicated video

YouTube has two common content formats for brand partnerships:

| Format | Description | Typical fee |
|---|---|---|
| Integration | Brand mention/use within a video that's about something else | $X per mention (lower) |
| Dedicated video | Whole video about the brand/product | $X×3-10 (higher) |
| Sponsor spot | 30-60 second pre-roll/mid-roll | $X×0.3 (much lower) |

The skill recommends the format based on tier + budget. Most ecommerce brands get more value from integrations than dedicated videos (more native, less commercial-feeling).

## Manager-mediated outreach

Many YouTube creators above 50K subscribers are managed. The skill detects manager email patterns (`management@`, `agency@`, `team@`) and adapts:

- Tone shifts to business-correspondence
- Includes media kit request / rate card request
- More structured deliverables ask
- Mentions the brand's previous influencer campaign experience (when relevant)

The skill recommends emailing the manager when one is listed even if the creator's personal email is also visible — managers screen and respond faster than creators do directly.
