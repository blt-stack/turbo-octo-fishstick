# Platform: Pinterest

Pinterest creator outreach is underused by most e-commerce brands. Pinterest has unique characteristics:
- Search-driven discovery (longer content lifespan than feed-based platforms)
- Visual-first
- High purchase intent (Pinterest users plan future buys)
- Strong fit for home, fashion, food, DIY, weddings, parenting

## Channel options

- **Pinterest message** (if creator has messaging on): primary in-platform
- **Email** (if in profile bio or linked website): preferred for serious partnerships
- **Pin comment** (rare for outreach; appropriate only as warm reply)

Pinterest's DM feature is less-used than other platforms; many creators don't check often. Email-first is typically higher-ROI for serious outreach.

## Tone

- **Nano**: warm + casual ("Hi [Name]!" — emoji ok)
- **Micro**: friendly professional
- **Mid-tier**: professional
- **Macro**: formal business

## Pinterest content format reality

Pinterest creators produce:
- Idea Pins (multi-image stories — Pinterest's TikTok-equivalent)
- Standard Pins (single image with link)
- Video Pins
- Boards (curated collections)

Brand partnerships on Pinterest typically:
- Brand-sponsored Idea Pins (Pinterest's version of an Instagram Reel partnership)
- Brand inclusion in curated boards
- Brand-featured DIY tutorials
- Brand visibility in seasonal/aspirational planning content (wedding boards, home renovation, holiday planning)

## Pinterest's content lifespan

Pins live and get rediscovered for months — sometimes years. A single Pin can drive traffic for 6-18 months.

This means:
- Outreach offer should reflect long-tail value (not just initial impressions)
- One sponsored Pin may have more ROI than 5 IG Stories
- Creators with strong evergreen-content track record are particularly valuable

The skill suggests this framing in mid-tier and macro outreach: "we value Pinterest specifically for the long-tail discovery your audience will engage with over months, not just launch week."

## Personalization on Pinterest

Reference specific boards or Pins:

> "Hi Sarah,
>
> Your 'Slow Saturday Mornings' board is exactly the aesthetic we built our brand around — especially the recent Pin showing the pour-over + journal setup. Would love to talk about collaborating on a Pin or Idea Pin featuring our pour-over."

Board references work particularly well on Pinterest because they show research depth.

## Required disclosures on Pinterest

- **Gifted only**: #gift or "Thanks to [brand]" in the Pin description
- **Hybrid / Paid**: "Paid partnership" disclosure in description, plus #ad
- **Affiliate**: #affiliate + Pinterest's affiliate link tools

Pinterest's enforcement is light but FTC rules apply.

## Outreach structure (Pinterest-specific)

```
Hi [First name],

[Product fit for their specific Pinterest content — 1-2 sentences]

[Offer — Pin sponsorship structure + payment + product — 2-3 sentences]

[Mention of Pinterest's long-tail value if relevant — 1 sentence]

[Soft ask + CTA — 1 sentence]

[Sign-off + name + brand]
```

## Common Pinterest outreach mistakes

- **Treating Pinterest like Instagram**: very different content formats and audience expectations
- **Asking for a single Pin without context for board strategy**: better to align with creator's board ecosystem
- **Missing the visual quality bar**: Pinterest creators care about aesthetic; subpar product photography kills outreach
- **Offering too-low rates**: Pinterest Pin rates are often lower per-Pin but the long-tail value is higher; underpaying = no response
- **Ignoring board context**: a brand that doesn't fit any of the creator's existing boards is a hard sell

## Pinterest-specific tactical recommendations

- **Pinterest is seasonal**: Q4 (holiday) and Q1 (resolutions, weddings) are highest-activity periods
- **Send messages Sunday-Thursday, 7-10 PM creator's time zone**: Pinterest planning behavior peaks evenings
- **Brand needs strong product photography**: Pinterest creators won't accept brand collabs that produce ugly Pins
- **Consider providing the imagery as part of the partnership**: many creators welcome brand-provided high-quality images they don't have to shoot themselves
- **Pinterest Trends data** (free public tool) shows seasonal interest patterns — mention specific trends in outreach to show you understand the platform
- **For mid-tier+, consider Pinterest's "Verified Merchant Program" + paid partnership tools**: more reliable than DM-based outreach

## When Pinterest outreach fits

Pinterest outreach makes most sense for brands in:
- Home + interior design
- Food + recipes
- Fashion + style
- Weddings + events
- Parenting + kids
- Beauty + skincare
- DIY + crafts
- Travel + planning

For brands that don't naturally fit "aspirational planning" content (B2B, productivity tools, consumables), Pinterest may not be the highest-ROI channel. The skill flags brand-Pinterest fit issues based on category.
