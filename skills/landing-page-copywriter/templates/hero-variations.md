# Hero Variations — The 3-Alternate Playbook

The hero is the single highest-leverage A/B test slot on any e-commerce page. A hero swap can move conversion 5-30% in either direction. Every run of this skill produces the main page hero plus three alternate heroes — each leading with a different appeal type so the buyer can test which angle their audience responds to.

The three alternates are:
1. **Problem-aware hero** — leads with the pain or frustration
2. **Outcome-aware hero** — leads with the result or transformation
3. **Social-proof-aware hero** — leads with credibility (numbers, press, expert)

Each alternate has the same structural elements (headline, sub-headline, visual brief, CTA) but a different opening angle.

---

## Why three angles, not three rewrites

Most "A/B test variants" are minor rewrites of the same hero — different word choice, different verb. These rarely move the needle because they test the *same argument*.

The three alternates here test fundamentally different *arguments*. The buyer learns whether their audience is moved more by pain, outcome, or credibility — and that finding generalizes beyond this one page.

---

## Alternate A: Problem-aware hero

**Use when:** the shopper has the problem and is searching for relief.

**Best for:** painkillers, supplements, fitness recovery, productivity tools, organization tools, anything tied to a known frustration.

**Headline pattern:** name the problem in the shopper's words.

**Sub-headline pattern:** specify the pain (when it shows up, what it costs them).

**Examples:**

> **Headline:** Your knees hurt every time you take the stairs.
> **Sub-headline:** Foam rollers help for an hour. The right insole helps for the day.
> **Visual brief:** Real photo of someone wincing on stairs, OR a close-up of an insole alongside a pair of foam rollers (showing scale and replacement-style)
> **CTA:** Try our insole risk-free

> **Headline:** Your inbox is a graveyard of "I'll respond tomorrow."
> **Sub-headline:** Our email triage flow gets you to inbox-zero in 20 minutes. Daily.
> **Visual brief:** Side-by-side: chaotic inbox (left) vs cleaned-up inbox (right). Or a single-screen device showing the after-state.
> **CTA:** Start triaging now

---

## Alternate B: Outcome-aware hero

**Use when:** the shopper aspires to a state they don't currently have.

**Best for:** lifestyle, beauty, home goods, hobbies, anything tied to a future version of the shopper's life.

**Headline pattern:** describe the after-state in sensory terms.

**Sub-headline pattern:** name what specifically gets the shopper to that state.

**Examples:**

> **Headline:** Wake up to soft, even skin — without a 12-step routine.
> **Sub-headline:** Three products, twice a day. Most people see real change in 14 days.
> **Visual brief:** Close-up portrait, natural light, no makeup, soft focus. The mood should feel calm, not promotional.
> **CTA:** Start the three-step

> **Headline:** Slow mornings start with the perfect cup.
> **Sub-headline:** Hand-thrown ceramic pour-over. One cup, three minutes, no machines.
> **Visual brief:** Overhead shot of the dripper on a wooden countertop, steam rising, soft morning light through a window.
> **CTA:** Shop the pour-over

---

## Alternate C: Social-proof-aware hero

**Use when:** the brand has real credibility (review count, notable customer, press mention, expert endorsement) and the category is one where shoppers are skeptical.

**Best for:** newer brands competing in mature categories, B2B tools, supplements, anything where the shopper would normally ask "but does it actually work."

**Headline pattern:** lead with the credibility marker.

**Sub-headline pattern:** state what it is and what specifically it does.

**Examples:**

> **Headline:** 12,000+ five-star reviews. Featured in NYT Wirecutter. Now in your inbox.
> **Sub-headline:** The ergonomic chair Wirecutter called "shockingly comfortable for the price." $399, ships free.
> **Visual brief:** Clean product shot, slight angle, with press logos along the bottom.
> **CTA:** Try it for 30 days

> **Headline:** Made for chefs who own actual knives. Used by 8 of NYC's top kitchens.
> **Sub-headline:** Hand-forged carbon steel that takes — and keeps — an edge most home knives can't.
> **Visual brief:** Knife laying across a wooden cutting board, with raw ingredients in the background. Optional: pull-quote from a named chef.
> **CTA:** Shop the knife

---

## Common pitfalls (across all three)

- **Mismatched angle for the brand.** A meditation app's social-proof hero shouldn't shout "10 MILLION DOWNLOADS." Match angle intensity to brand voice.
- **Vague headlines.** "Better sleep starts here" → too generic. "Fall asleep in under 12 minutes" → specific and testable.
- **Sub-headlines that just repeat the headline.** Each line has a job; don't waste either.
- **Visual brief that's a stock photo.** Specify a real, brand-aligned image. Stock photos kill all three angles.
- **CTAs that don't match the angle.** A pain-led headline shouldn't end in "Learn more." End in "Get relief now."

---

## Variant naming convention

In the .docx brief and the HTML output, the three alternates are labeled:

- `Hero A — Problem-aware`
- `Hero B — Outcome-aware`
- `Hero C — Social-proof-aware`

This lets the buyer reference them clearly in A/B testing tools (Google Optimize, Convert.com, Shopify Experiments).

---

## Suggested A/B test order

If the buyer asks "which alternate should I test first?", recommend based on what they currently have:

- Current hero is feature-led / generic → test **Hero B (Outcome-aware)** first
- Current hero is problem-led already → test **Hero C (Social-proof-aware)** to layer credibility
- Current hero is social-proof-led → test **Hero A (Problem-aware)** to see if pain converts harder for cold traffic
- Brand has no current hero (greenfield page) → launch with **Hero B (Outcome-aware)** as default; A/B test **Hero A (Problem-aware)** next

Test for 14-21 days (depending on traffic volume) before declaring a winner. Don't peek at results in the first 3 days — early data is noisy.

---

## When the buyer wants only the hero block

Sometimes the buyer doesn't need a full landing page — just a hero rewrite for an A/B test. Honor that. Skip the rest of the page architecture. Output:

- Main hero (1 polished version)
- Hero A (Problem-aware)
- Hero B (Outcome-aware)
- Hero C (Social-proof-aware)
- A/B test recommendation

Document this as a valid use of the skill in the brief. Buyers iterating on heroes don't need to re-do the whole page each time.
