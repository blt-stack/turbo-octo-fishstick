# Collection / Category Page Architecture

Collection pages get massive traffic but are often default Shopify templates with no real copy. This is one of the most under-utilized conversion levers in e-commerce. A well-written collection page does three things: tells the shopper which products in the collection fit them, builds confidence in the category, and routes them to the right PDP fast.

The architecture is simpler than a PDP — most of the page is the product grid — but the surrounding copy is what makes a collection page convert.

---

## Block sequence (top to bottom)

1. **Sticky announcement bar** (optional, same as PDP)
2. **Collection hero** — collection name, positioning statement, hero image
3. **Filter labels + copy** (when categories have meaningful filter dimensions)
4. **Top-of-grid copy** — 1-2 paragraphs of context above the product grid
5. **Product grid** (handled by Shopify, but the skill specifies grid tone of voice for product cards)
6. **Mid-grid content break** (optional — a story block, comparison guide, or expert recommendation between rows)
7. **Below-grid copy** — collection-level FAQ, buying guide, or category-level social proof
8. **Cross-collection nav** — related collections to explore
9. **Trust footer** (same as PDP)

---

## Block 1: Sticky announcement bar (optional)

Same as PDP. Only include if there's a live promo relevant to the collection.

---

## Block 2: Collection hero

The collection-page equivalent of a PDP hero. Six elements:

- **Collection name** — already known but should be displayed prominently as the H1
- **Positioning statement** — 1-2 sentences. What this collection is for, who it's for, and why it exists as a curated set rather than scattered SKUs.
- **Hero image** — recommended 2000×800px, lifestyle/in-use rather than catalog
- **Optional secondary CTAs** — link to a buying guide, a quiz, or a curated bundle
- **Quick stat** — "127 items" or "12 bestsellers" (signals depth)

**Conversion job:** in 3 seconds, the shopper knows whether this collection contains what they came for and whether they're in the right place.

**Pitfall:** Shopify's default collection hero is often a vague description ("Our Bestsellers Collection") that adds nothing. Replace it with a positioning statement that names the buyer.

**Length targets:**
- Positioning statement: 15-30 words

---

## Block 3: Filter labels + copy

If the collection has filters (size, color, price, use case, fit, etc.), the filter labels themselves should be written with care.

**For each filter dimension:**
- Filter name (e.g., "Fit", "Use case", "Skill level")
- 1-line description on hover or as helper text (e.g., "For beginners or pros?")
- Filter options with short, scannable labels

**Conversion job:** the shopper narrows the grid down to their fit, which dramatically lifts both add-to-cart rate and per-session revenue.

**Pitfall:** filters labeled "Material A / Material B" instead of "Cotton / Linen" — keep human language.

---

## Block 4: Top-of-grid copy

1-2 short paragraphs immediately above the product grid. Different purpose from the hero — the hero positions the collection; this block guides the shopper *into the grid*.

**Useful patterns:**
- Curator note ("Here's what we'd pick first, and why") — useful for boutique stores
- Buying-guide pointer ("New to [category]? Start with our [bundle / guide / starter pack]")
- Promise + commitment ("Every item is tested by us before it goes live. If you don't love it, we'll take it back.")
- Expert framing ("Our knife sharpening category covers three sharpening methods — read which one fits your kitchen below")

**Length:** 60-150 words total.

**Conversion job:** orient the shopper before they start scanning. Reduces the "I don't know what to click" dropout.

---

## Block 5: Product grid (specified, not designed)

This block is rendered by Shopify. The skill specifies what each product card should contain:

- Product photo (consistent aspect ratio across the collection — typically 1:1 or 4:5)
- Product name (in brand voice)
- Price (regular + sale, if applicable)
- Star rating + review count (when available)
- Quick-add or Quick-view button
- Optional: variant swatches (color/size dots) so shoppers don't have to click into the PDP to see options

**Card tone of voice:** match brand voice but err on the side of brevity. Product names should be scannable; if names are too long, recommend renaming them in the buyer's Shopify catalog (and offer to do that via the Listing SEO Optimizer skill).

---

## Block 6: Mid-grid content break (optional)

Most stores skip this; the best stores don't. After the first 4-8 products in the grid, insert a content block that breaks the visual rhythm and adds value.

**Options:**
- **Story block** — short paragraph + image, often the brand's POV on the category
- **Buying guide** — "How to pick the right [category] for you" with 3-5 decision criteria
- **Bundle recommendation** — "Save 15% on the Starter Bundle"
- **Comparison module** — quick "Beginner vs Pro" or "Standard vs Premium" snippet linking to a deeper compare page
- **Expert recommendation** — "Our buyer's top pick for [use case]"

After the content break, the grid resumes.

**Pitfall:** breaking the grid with a banner ad — feels mass-market and lowers premium feel. Use editorial content, not promo banners.

---

## Block 7: Below-grid copy

After the product grid, the shopper has either bought, clicked into a PDP, or is about to leave. This block tries to capture the leaving shoppers.

**Options:**
- **Collection-level FAQ** (4-6 questions) — common questions about the category, not specific products
- **Buyer's guide** — long-form (300-500 words) helping the shopper choose. Good for SEO too.
- **Category-level social proof** — aggregate review count across the collection, customer photos pulled from across the category
- **Newsletter capture** — "New to [category]? Get our weekly picks." with email opt-in

**Length:** 200-600 words total.

**Conversion job:** rescue the leaving shopper with either decision support (FAQ, guide) or relationship capture (newsletter).

---

## Block 8: Cross-collection nav

3-5 related collections. Same format as PDP cross-sells but at the collection level.

**Selection criteria:**
- Adjacent categories (e.g., from a coffee collection → a teaware collection)
- Audience overlap (e.g., from a minimalist desk collection → a home office lighting collection)

---

## Block 9: Trust footer

Same as PDP.

---

## Length targets (whole page)

- Hero positioning: 15-30 words
- Top-of-grid copy: 60-150 words
- Mid-grid break (if used): 80-200 words
- Below-grid copy: 200-600 words
- Total non-grid copy: 350-1000 words

Collection pages with under 300 words of non-grid copy underperform on SEO. Pages with over 1500 words of non-grid copy start to feel like the grid is buried — find the balance.

## Mobile considerations

Collection pages on mobile are mostly grid; the surrounding copy compresses heavily.

- Hero stacks (image above, copy below)
- Filters become a collapsible drawer
- Mid-grid breaks remain full-width
- Below-grid copy gets its own collapsible section ("Read more")

## Common pitfalls

- **Empty hero** — collection page uses Shopify's default and skips the positioning statement entirely.
- **No copy above the grid** — shoppers land, see the grid, and don't know how to think about it.
- **Generic FAQ** — using PDP-level questions in collection FAQ. Collection FAQ should be about the category, not a specific product.
- **Over-faceted filters** — 12+ filter dimensions paralyzes shoppers. Keep to 4-6 most useful.
- **No mid-grid break** — the page feels like an undifferentiated catalog dump.
