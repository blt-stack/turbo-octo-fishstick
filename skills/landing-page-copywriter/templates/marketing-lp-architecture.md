# Marketing Landing Page Architecture

Marketing LPs are different from PDPs in three ways:

1. **One job.** A PDP serves many shopper journeys (research, comparison, repeat purchase). A marketing LP serves one — typically a paid-traffic visitor with a specific intent (BFCM, product launch, ad-driven, influencer campaign).
2. **No competing CTAs.** No top nav. No related products. No "explore the collection." The shopper either takes the one action or leaves.
3. **More objection-handling.** Paid traffic is colder than organic — the shopper doesn't know the brand. Every block has to overcome that.

This architecture is for the cold-traffic destination LP: ad clicks, influencer link clicks, email-campaign landings, affiliate links.

---

## Block sequence (top to bottom)

1. **Minimal header** — logo + tiny trust signal (no nav)
2. **Hero** — outcome-led, with one big CTA
3. **The promise** — what specifically will happen if they take the action
4. **Social proof — early** (logos / press / aggregate ratings)
5. **The product / offer**
6. **Objection-handling block** (3-4 most common objections)
7. **Detailed product walkthrough**
8. **Featured reviews / case studies**
9. **Comparison / "Why us"** (optional, when there's a clear incumbent)
10. **Risk reversal** (guarantee, returns, terms)
11. **FAQ** (4-6 conversion-focused questions)
12. **Final purchase block** — big CTA, restated offer
13. **Minimal footer** — legal, contact, no other links

---

## Block 1: Minimal header

No top navigation. Just:

- Brand logo (links to home or to the LP itself — not to other store pages)
- One tiny trust signal (e.g., "★★★★★ 4.8 / 12K reviews", "As seen in [press]")

**Pitfall:** including the full store nav. The shopper clicks "Shop All" out of habit, lands on the homepage, gets distracted, and leaves. Cold-traffic LPs lose 20-40% of conversions to full navs.

---

## Block 2: Hero (outcome-led)

The marketing-LP hero is more aggressive than a PDP hero. Six elements:

- **Headline** — outcome the shopper will get (8-14 words)
- **Sub-headline** — the specific mechanism (15-25 words)
- **Hero image or short video** — typically lifestyle / transformation / in-use
- **Primary CTA** — verb-led, urgent ("Get [outcome] now", "Try it free for 30 days")
- **Trust signal under the CTA** — "Free shipping · 30-day refund · No subscription"
- **Optional: scarcity element** — only if genuine (e.g., "Launch pricing ends Dec 18")

**Conversion job:** the shopper just clicked an ad. In 3 seconds they need to confirm the ad's promise is real, and have a clear action to take.

**Pitfall:** mismatch between ad promise and hero. If the ad said "Cure your back pain in 7 days" and the LP hero says "Premium ergonomic chair", the shopper leaves immediately. The hero has to *continue the ad's promise*.

---

## Block 3: The promise

A short section restating exactly what will happen if the shopper takes the action.

**Format:**
- Headline ("Here's what you get when you [action]")
- 3-5 bullets, each describing a specific outcome
- Each bullet ≤15 words

**Example:**

> Here's what you get when you start The 30-Day Pour-Over Practice:
> - Three coffees a day, all hand-brewed, in under 4 minutes each
> - Our 12-page brewing guide, written for beginners
> - The ceramic dripper, mesh filter, and brew journal — shipped Friday
> - A 30-day money-back promise, no questions

**Conversion job:** translate the offer into concrete deliverables the shopper can mentally check off.

---

## Block 4: Social proof — early

Cold-traffic LPs need credibility *before* the product details. Most LPs put social proof at the bottom — wrong.

**Elements:**
- Aggregate star rating + review count
- Press logos (3-5 publications, only real ones)
- Notable customer logos or quote (B2C: influencer; B2B: a recognizable brand)

This is a thin strip — not a full social-proof section yet. That comes later.

---

## Block 5: The product / offer

Now the shopper knows the outcome and trusts the brand a bit. Show them what they're buying.

**Elements:**
- Product image (different angle from hero — often a top-down or unboxing shot)
- Product name + one-line description
- What's in the box (3-6 line items)
- Price (with original price struck-through if there's a promo)
- CTA — same as hero or slightly different copy

**Conversion job:** make the offer concrete. Cold-traffic shoppers fear they'll pay and get less than promised. Itemize.

---

## Block 6: Objection-handling block

The 3-4 most common reasons a shopper says no, with responses.

**Format:**
- Sub-header: "But what about..."
- Each objection presented as a question
- 2-3 sentence response per objection

**Common objections to handle:**
- "Will this work for someone like me?" → niche-specific reassurance
- "How is this different from [common alternative]?" → 1-2 sentence differentiator
- "What if I don't like it?" → return policy preview
- "How long does shipping take?" → shipping promise
- "Is this hard to use?" → simplicity claim

**Conversion job:** preempt the doubts that would otherwise cause the shopper to leave to research.

---

## Block 7: Detailed product walkthrough

Same idea as PDP walkthrough — features + photos. Tighter here because LP shoppers have shorter attention spans.

3-5 features max. Each feature has:
- Photo or diagram
- Feature headline (3-5 words)
- Description (20-30 words — shorter than PDP)

---

## Block 8: Featured reviews / case studies

Now that the shopper has the details, give them the real-customer validation.

**Elements:**
- 2-3 featured reviews with photo, name, headline, body text
- Optional: short video testimonial (60-90 seconds)
- For B2B or high-ticket: 1-2 mini case studies with before/after

**Selection criteria for marketing LP:**
- Lean harder on transformation/outcome reviews than typical PDP reviews
- One review that specifically addresses the most common objection

---

## Block 9: Comparison / "Why us" (optional)

Include when:
- The buyer is competing against a well-known incumbent
- The buyer has a meaningful price or feature advantage

Skip when:
- The brand position is "we're not for everyone" (premium, niche)
- There's no clear comparable

**Format:** 2-3 columns (us vs them). Lean on the buyer's strongest dimensions. Stay honest — exaggerated comparisons read as defensive.

---

## Block 10: Risk reversal

The single most important block for cold traffic. Take the financial risk off the shopper.

**Elements:**
- Big visual (a guarantee badge or a money-back-promise illustration)
- Headline: "[X]-day money-back guarantee"
- Sub-text: 2-3 sentences explaining the promise in plain language
- Optional: "If you don't [outcome], we'll refund you — no questions, no hoops."

**Conversion job:** the shopper's brain frames the purchase as risky. Risk reversal flips that — now the *not buying* feels risky (missing the outcome).

**Pitfall:** vague guarantees ("satisfaction guaranteed") — useless. Be specific. Give a duration, give a process, give a no-loophole promise.

---

## Block 11: FAQ

4-6 conversion-focused questions. Different selection criteria than PDP FAQ:

- Skip product trivia
- Cover: payment options, shipping speed, return process, "does this come with..." (the rest-of-package questions), "how is this charged" (subscription anxieties), "what if I have a problem"
- One question that handles the price objection ("Why is this $X? What makes it worth it?")

---

## Block 12: Final purchase block

The closing argument. Big and unmissable.

**Elements:**
- Restated outcome (rephrase the headline, don't repeat verbatim)
- Restated offer (what's included)
- Restated price
- Big CTA
- Final trust signals: guarantee + shipping + payment options visible

**Pitfall:** introducing new information in the closer. The closer summarizes — never opens new arguments.

---

## Block 13: Minimal footer

Legal + contact only. No store nav.

- Brand logo (small)
- Contact email or chat
- Terms / Privacy / Returns links
- Copyright

No "Shop All" link. No collection nav. No "About Us" link. This is a single-purpose page.

---

## Length targets

- Total word count: 800-1800 words
- Lower-ticket (<$30): 600-1000 words
- Mid-ticket ($30-150): 800-1500 words
- Higher-ticket ($150+): 1200-2500 words

Marketing LPs are typically *longer* than PDPs because they have to do more objection-handling work.

## Mobile

LP traffic is 70-85% mobile. Mobile-specific notes:

- Hero image stacks above headline
- Comparison table converts to side-by-side cards or vertical stack
- CTAs are full-width
- Sticky CTA bar at the bottom of viewport (always visible)
- Image carousels work better than image grids
- Long-form copy gets section "Read more" toggles only if absolutely necessary (most readers won't expand)

## Common pitfalls

- **Full store nav at the top** — kills conversion. Use a minimal header.
- **CTA mismatch with the ad** — different verb, different promise, instant trust break.
- **No risk reversal** — cold shoppers won't take a leap of faith without one.
- **Burying social proof at the bottom** — show it early *and* late.
- **Hard scarcity that isn't real** ("Only 3 left!" when there are 300) — Gen Z shoppers smell this and screenshot it. Brand damage.
- **No second CTA placement** — the CTA needs to be visible whenever the shopper is ready, not just at the top and bottom.
