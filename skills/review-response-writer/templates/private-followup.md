# Private Follow-up Drafts

When the public reply hands off to a private channel ("I just emailed you"), the skill drafts the private email/DM the buyer sends after the public reply goes live.

The public reply is **for future readers.** The private reply is **for the actual customer.**

## When the private follow-up is drafted

| Tier | Follow-up? |
|---|---|
| 1-2★ Recovery | Almost always |
| 3★ Clarify (with fix offer) | Yes |
| 3★ Clarify (probe only) | Only if customer responds to public reply |
| 4-5★ Amplify | Rarely — only if the public reply seeded a "DM us" action |
| Abusive | Yes — but more cautious, fact-focused |
| Fake/competitor | No — there is no real customer to email |
| Sycophantic | No |

## Structure

The private follow-up is warmer, longer, and more concrete than the public reply:

```
Subject: [Subject referencing the review or order]

Hi [First name],

[Acknowledge their review briefly — assume they already read your public reply, so don't re-apologize at length.]

Here's what we're doing:
- [Concrete action 1]
- [Concrete action 2]
- [Timeline]

[Any next step from the customer's side — e.g., "Reply with your shipping address" or "No action needed from you, we have everything."]

[Optional: a sentence that opens the door for ongoing feedback without forcing it.]

Thank you again for telling us. It matters.

— [First name]
[Role / title]
[Email signature]
```

## Subject line patterns

- Recovery: `Following up on your review — order #[id]`
- Recovery (high-stakes): `Personal note from [founder name] — [brand]`
- Clarify: `Quick follow-up to your review`
- Amplify: `Thank you for the [N]-star review` (rare; only when DM/email handoff was seeded)

Never use:
- "URGENT" or "ACTION REQUIRED" (heavy-handed)
- Order numbers as the entire subject (impersonal)
- Generic "Thank you for your feedback" (form-letter)

## Concrete-action lists

The body's middle section should be a bulleted list of what the brand is *doing*, not what the brand is *feeling*. The customer already knows the brand is sorry — what they want to see is action.

Examples:

### Shipping refund + reship
```
Here's what we're doing:
- Full refund processed today (you'll see it in 3-5 business days)
- New replacement going out via FedEx Overnight at no charge
- Tracking will be sent the moment it ships (today by 5pm ET)

No action needed from your end — we have everything we need.
```

### Defect replacement
```
Here's what we're doing:
- Replacement [product] going out today, expedited shipping
- You don't need to return the original — keep it or pass it on
- I'm personally inspecting current inventory of this batch tomorrow

You'll get tracking by tomorrow morning.
```

### Sizing swap
```
Here's what we're doing:
- Sending a [size] in [color] at no charge
- You can keep the original — no need to return
- Shipping out today

If [size] doesn't fit either, just let me know and we'll keep trying.
```

### CS failure recovery
```
Here's what we're doing:
- I'm personally handling this from here
- [The fix the customer originally asked for] is in motion as of today
- I've added you to a flag that routes future emails directly to me

I'm so sorry it took a public review to get this resolved. That's on me.
```

## Tone calibration

The private email is warmer than the public reply. The brand can:
- Use "I" more freely
- Apologize a second time if it fits (the public reply was for the audience; the private reply is for the customer)
- Mention specifics that would be inappropriate publicly ("I saw your previous order from January")
- Use the customer's order number, tracking number, full email
- Sign with title and contact info

The private email should **not**:
- Reference the review's star count ("Sorry you left a 1-star review")
- Ask the customer to change/update the review
- Promise things that exceed authority
- Sound legal/formal — even in defect cases, write like a person

## When the buyer wants the founder to send the private follow-up

For high-stakes recoveries (high-LTV customer, public complaint risk, influencer/press), the skill drafts the private follow-up under the founder's name with founder voice:

```
Hi [Name],

Jamie here — founder of [brand]. I saw your review and wanted to write personally.

[Brief acknowledgment]

[The concrete action list]

If you'd be open to a quick 10-minute call this week, I'd value the chance to hear what we got wrong and what we could do better. No expectations on your end — just an offer.

[Direct contact]

— Jamie
```

The founder offer to call is high-value and low-cost. Use it sparingly (high-LTV or high-public-visibility cases) so it stays special.

## Edge case: customer hasn't responded to public reply

If the buyer sets up a scheduled follow-up workflow (out of scope for this skill but common), the rule is: send the private follow-up email **within 24 hours of the public reply going live, regardless of whether the customer asked**.

The 24-hour rule matters because:
- The customer is most engaged in the first day
- Future readers checking the public reply will see "we just emailed you" — and a fast follow-through builds trust
- Delays compound the bad-experience perception

## Edge case: customer responds publicly to the brand's public reply

If the customer escalates publicly ("Your reply doesn't help, I want a refund NOW"), the brand should:
1. Reply once more publicly, very briefly: "Replying to your email now."
2. Send the private follow-up immediately.
3. Stop public back-and-forth. Long public threads always hurt the brand.

The private follow-up in this case should be even more action-forward and even less defensive.
