# Tier: Recovery (1★, 2★)

A 1-2★ review is rarely about the rating — it's about the buyer's audience. Every future shopper reading the product page will see this review *and* the brand's reply. The reply is for them, not for the reviewer.

## Reply mode

1. **Acknowledge the specific failure** — not "we're sorry you had a bad experience," but "we're sorry the kettle arrived with a dented base."
2. **Don't argue, even when you could win** — public disputes lose even when they're right.
3. **State the concrete fix** in one sentence — replacement / refund / inspection / engineering fix.
4. **Hand off to a private channel** — "We just emailed you" or "Please reply to this thread" or "DM us at [handle]."
5. **Sign with a real first name**, not "the team."

## The 4-part recovery skeleton

```
Hi [First name],

Thank you for taking the time to share this — and we're sorry [specific issue in their words].

[Concrete fix in one sentence: we're sending a replacement / we issued a full refund yesterday / we've flagged this with our [team] and are inspecting current inventory.]

I just sent you an email so we can [verb] this directly. We want to make this right.

— [First name], [Role]
```

## When the customer is partially or fully wrong

The customer is sometimes wrong about a fact (e.g., they ordered the wrong size, or the product they reviewed is from a different brand entirely). Public replies still don't argue. Reframe gently:

> "It looks like the size you received was Medium — we'd love to swap that for a Large at no charge. I just emailed you."

Never write "Actually, you ordered the wrong size." Never write "Per our return policy." Never imply the customer didn't read the listing.

## Offline channel handoff scripts

Pick the channel the buyer already operates well:

- **Email handoff** — "I just emailed you so we can take care of this directly."
- **DM handoff** (Instagram/Facebook brands) — "Please DM us at @[handle] so we can help."
- **Helpdesk handoff** — "I just opened a ticket — you'll see a reply from us in the next hour."
- **Direct ticket reply** (Trustpilot, Google) — "Please reply to this review with your order number and I'll personally follow up."

The handoff is non-optional for 1-2★ replies. Without it, the reply reads as performative.

## Issue-category-specific recovery patterns

### Shipping complaint

```
Hi [Name],

I'm so sorry — a [N]-day wait isn't what we promised, and that's on us.
I checked the tracking and your package [status]. We've issued a [partial refund / shipping credit / full refund + new replacement] as of [date].
I just emailed you the details. Thank you for your patience.

— [Name]
```

### Product defect

```
Hi [Name],

A [defect description] is not the experience we want anyone to have with [product].
We're sending a replacement out today via expedited shipping, and we've flagged this batch internally to make sure it doesn't happen to anyone else.
I just emailed you to confirm the shipping address. Thank you for letting us know.

— [Name]
```

### Sizing / fit mismatch

```
Hi [Name],

We're sorry the fit didn't work for you — sizing apparel for every body is something we're still learning.
We'd love to send you a [Larger/Smaller] at no charge — and you can keep the original.
I just emailed you to set this up.

— [Name]
```

### Customer service failure

```
Hi [Name],

[N] days without a response isn't acceptable, and I'm sorry it took a public review for us to surface this.
We've issued the [refund/replacement] you asked for, and I personally followed up via email so we can close this out.

— [Name], [Role/Founder]
```

When the CS failure is what triggered the review, escalate to founder reply tone — a junior CS person responding to a CS failure undermines the apology.

### Expectation mismatch (looked different / didn't perform as expected)

```
Hi [Name],

We're sorry [product] didn't land the way you hoped — that mismatch is real and we don't want anyone feeling stuck with it.
We've issued a full refund as of today, no need to return [product] [or: please return when convenient].
I emailed the confirmation. Thank you for the honest feedback — we'll use it.

— [Name]
```

### Pricing / value complaint

This one is delicate. Don't litigate price publicly.

```
Hi [Name],

Thanks for the honest feedback. Price-to-value is a personal call, and we'd rather you felt great about the purchase than locked in.
I just emailed you about a refund.

— [Name]
```

Never explain why the product costs what it costs. Never compare to competitors. Never defend the price.

## Founder-escalation triggers

Recommend the reply be sent under the founder's name (not a CS rep's) when any of these apply:

- The reviewer is high-LTV (3+ orders or $500+ lifetime spend)
- The reviewer mentions a public complaint or threat ("I'm reporting this to ...")
- The reviewer is an influencer, press, or industry contact
- The reviewer's complaint is a systemic issue affecting multiple customers
- The platform is Trustpilot or Google (heavy SEO weight)
- The reviewer's tone is reasonable + the issue is real (don't waste a founder reply on an abusive reviewer)

When founder escalation is recommended, the reply should mention the founder's first name, not "the founder" — e.g., "I'm Jamie, the founder — I read every review."

## What NEVER goes in a recovery reply

- "We're sorry you feel that way" (gaslighting language — apologize for the thing, not their feelings)
- "Per our policy" or "Our terms of service state" (hiding behind policy)
- "We have thousands of happy customers" (dismissive)
- "If you had contacted us first, we could have ..." (blaming the customer for going public)
- Discount codes ("Use SORRY10 next time" — looks like buying off the review, and on Amazon it violates TOS)
- Defensive explanations of why the problem happened (the customer doesn't care; future readers care less)
- Multiple paragraphs of apology (one is plenty; brevity reads as confidence)
- The customer's full email address or order ID (privacy)
