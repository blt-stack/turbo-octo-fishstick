# Tier: Clarify (3★)

3★ is the highest-signal rating in the review system. The customer almost loved it. One specific thing got in the way. Identify and fix that thing — and you'll never need to win the customer back, because they're already 70% there.

## Reply mode

1. **Specific thanks for the parts they did like** — proves you read the review.
2. **Reflect the friction in their words** — name the specific thing that knocked off two stars.
3. **Probe for what would have made it 5★** — the answer is your roadmap.
4. **Light fix offer** (when appropriate) — replacement / refund / new sizing / a quick how-to — but not always. 3★ doesn't necessarily warrant a refund; it warrants attention.
5. **Sign with a real first name.**

## The 4-part clarify skeleton

```
Hi [First name],

Thank you for the honest feedback — and especially for [specific positive they mentioned].

I'm curious about [the specific friction]. [Probe question: was it X, or Y, or something else? / Did the [issue] start right away or develop over time?]

[Optional light fix: We'd love to send you a [replacement / different size / something] if that would help.]

If you have a minute, [DM us / reply to this / email us at hello@brand.com] — we'd love to figure out what would have made it 5 stars.

— [First name]
```

## When to offer a fix vs. when to just probe

| Situation | Offer fix? |
|---|---|
| Customer mentioned a defect, even minor | Yes — replacement or partial refund |
| Customer mentioned wrong sizing | Yes — swap at no cost |
| Customer mentioned an issue you've already fixed in a newer batch | Maybe — offer the new batch as a replacement |
| Customer mentioned an experience friction (slow shipping, missing card) | Yes — small credit or refund |
| Customer's friction is preference-based ("I wished it came in green") | No — thank them, log it, move on |
| Customer mentioned a competitor as preferable | No — never engage with the comparison |
| Customer's friction is genuinely a misuse / misunderstanding | Educate gently in the reply — don't offer a fix |

## Probe questions by friction category

The probe is the highest-value line in a 3★ reply. Some templates:

### Vague disappointment ("It was fine, but not great")
> "I'd love to know more — was it the [feature A], [feature B], or something specific about [the use case]? Even a one-line note helps us improve."

### Specific feature missing
> "Totally hear you on [missing thing] — that's on our list. Out of curiosity, would [adjacent feature we already have] have helped, or is [missing thing] the deal-breaker?"

### Quality concern
> "Did [issue] happen right away, or develop over time? That detail helps us narrow down whether it's a manufacturing batch issue or a usage pattern."

### Comparison to expectation
> "The mismatch between the listing photo and the real product is something we're working on. Was it the [color / size / texture / scale] that surprised you most?"

### Mixed verdict
> "It sounds like you liked [thing] but [other thing] held it back. If [other thing] were fixed, would this be a 5-star product for you? We're working on [related improvement] and your reply would help us prioritize."

## Reading the body, not the stars

A "4★ that reads like a 3★" — lots of constructive criticism with one positive sentence — gets the clarify template even though the rating is 4. Likewise, a "3★ that reads like a 5★" (effusive praise but they reflexively picked 3) gets the amplify template.

The body tells you what the customer actually thinks. The star is just where their finger landed.

## What NEVER goes in a clarify reply

- A scripted "Thank you for your 3-star review!" (3-star is awkward to say back to them)
- A discount code (cheapens the conversation)
- A defensive explanation
- Asking the customer to update the review ("If you could change your rating to 5 stars...") — violates Amazon TOS, looks needy on every platform
- Multiple probe questions (one is enough; a list reads as interrogation)
- A long apology (the customer is not that upset — they gave you 3 stars, not 1)
