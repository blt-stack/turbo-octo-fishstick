# Defects / Damages / Quality Complaints — Reply Patterns + Action Recommendations

The highest-stakes category. The customer paid for a working product and didn't get one. Both the fix AND the relationship matter — a fast refund handled coldly loses the customer for life; a slower fix handled warmly often keeps them.

Defects are also the most public-risk category — angry customers post product defects on social media, review sites, and DMs to other customers. Handling them well is risk management.

## Sub-types and how to identify them

| Sub-type | Identifier signals |
|---|---|
| **Broken on arrival (BOA)** | "Arrived broken"; "shattered in the box"; "doesn't work out of the box" |
| **Broke after N days** | "Worked for [N days/weeks/months] then stopped"; durability complaint |
| **Manufacturing defect** | "Stitching is unraveling"; "color is uneven"; "fit doesn't match the size chart"; "ingredients are off" |
| **Color / material mismatch** | "The color in the photo isn't the color I got"; "feels different than the description" |
| **Use-causes-damage** | "It broke when I [used it normally]"; "fell apart on first use" |
| **Smell / sensory** | "Has a chemical smell"; "tastes off" — common for consumables, candles, certain materials |

## Per-sub-type reply patterns + action recommendations

### Broken on arrival (BOA)

**Reply pattern:**
```
Hi [name],

I'm so sorry your [product] arrived broken. That's a real disappointment and
it's our problem, not yours.

[Resolve fast]
I'm shipping you a replacement [product] today via priority — it should
arrive within [X business days]. You don't need to send anything back; please
just dispose of the broken one (or take a quick photo first if it's easy —
helps us with the carrier's damage claim).

And here's a $[10-20] store credit for the trouble: [code]. No need to use
it right away.

[Close warmly]
Thanks for letting us know. If anything else comes up, just reply to this
email — it comes straight to me.

— [Founder name]
```

**Recommended actions:**
- Default: free replacement (no return required) + small store credit
- For high-value items (>$200): replacement + credit + photo request for insurance
- Track all BOA reports — if 3+ for the same product in 30 days, that's a packaging or fragility issue worth fixing at the source

### Broke after N days

**Reply pattern (within warranty period):**
```
Hi [name],

I'm sorry your [product] broke after [N] days. That's not the durability we
build for, and we'll make it right.

[Resolve]
I'll ship a replacement today. If you can send a quick photo of the failure
point, that helps us figure out if this was a one-off or something we should
look at across the batch.

You can keep the broken one or recycle it — no need to ship back.

[Close]
Thanks for telling us.

— [Founder name]
```

**Recommended actions:**
- Within warranty (most brands: 30-90 days for use-defects): replacement at no charge
- Slightly past warranty (90-180 days): case-by-case — often replacement at cost or significant discount on replacement
- Well past warranty (1+ year): explain warranty terms warmly, offer discount on replacement
- For pattern detection: same product breaking same way for 3+ customers in 30 days = systemic defect, escalate

### Manufacturing defect

**Reply pattern:**
```
Hi [name],

That's a manufacturing issue — sorry it slipped past QC. Sending a replacement
today.

[Resolve]
The replacement will go out within [X business days] via priority. You don't
need to return the defective one.

If you can take a quick photo of the defect (just one or two angles), it
helps us flag the batch internally and improve QC. Totally optional.

[Close]
Thanks for catching this.

— [Founder name]
```

**Recommended actions:**
- Default: free replacement + photo request (for QC, not for the customer)
- For minor manufacturing defects the customer wants to keep: partial refund (typically 20-40% of price)
- Flag the batch internally — if QC missed this defect on one unit, it may be missing it on others

### Color / material mismatch

**Reply pattern:**
```
Hi [name],

I'm sorry the [product] doesn't match what you expected from the photos. Color
on screen vs in-hand is hard, but it shouldn't be wildly off.

[Resolve options]
A few options:
1) Full refund if you'd rather return it (I'll send a return label)
2) Exchange for a different color/variant
3) Keep it + partial refund of [$X] if it still works for you despite the mismatch

What works best?

[Close]
And if you have a sec to send a quick photo of the actual vs the listing
photo, it helps us tune our color rendering for future customers.

— [Founder name]
```

**Recommended actions:**
- Customer's choice: refund, exchange, or partial refund + keep
- For category-wide color-rendering issues (multiple customers reporting): consider updating product photos at the source
- Don't push the partial-refund route too hard — feels like a bargain when the customer expected the product as advertised

### Use-causes-damage

**Reply pattern:**
```
Hi [name],

I'm sorry your [product] broke when you [used it]. That's frustrating.

[Investigate before acting]
Quick question — can you tell me a bit more about what happened? Specifically:
- What were you doing when it broke?
- How long had you had it?
- Was it the [part X] or [part Y] that failed?

[Set expectation]
Depending on what happened, here's what I can do:
- If it's a manufacturing issue (broke under normal use), we'll replace it
  for free.
- If it broke under conditions beyond normal use, I can offer a 30% discount
  on a replacement.

Either way, I want to figure this out with you.

— [Founder name]
```

**Recommended actions:**
- Investigate before deciding action — the brand's response depends on whether it's manufacturing or misuse
- Default to giving the customer benefit of the doubt unless evidence clearly points to misuse
- For products with known fragility (ceramic, glass, electronics): note in the reply when normal use vs misuse is genuinely ambiguous

### Smell / sensory issues (consumables, candles, materials)

**Reply pattern:**
```
Hi [name],

That's not how [product] should smell/taste. Sorry it landed wrong.

[Resolve]
Sending a replacement from a different batch today — should arrive within
[X business days]. You don't need to send the original back; please just
dispose of it.

If the replacement has the same issue, that's a batch-level problem and I
want to know immediately — just reply to this email and I'll dig in.

— [Founder name]
```

**Recommended actions:**
- Default: replacement from different batch + identify the batch the original was from (for internal tracking)
- For consumables (food, supplements): consider testing the original batch for contamination if multiple complaints
- For candles: usually a fragrance-oil batch issue; replacement from new batch fixes
- If brand has subscription customers on the affected batch: proactive outreach is wise (don't wait for them to complain)

## Sentiment overrides for defects

Defects skew toward angry. Customers feel cheated. Tone:

| Sentiment | Adjustments |
|---|---|
| Angry | Lead with strong apology, faster action (don't ask permission — fix it), larger credit, founder personally signed, optional founder-call followup |
| Neutral | Standard pattern works — customer is being reasonable; match the energy |
| Friendly (customer apologizes for complaining) | Match the warmth, the customer is making it easy — still fix the underlying issue fully |

---

## Common pitfalls in defects replies

- **Asking the customer to ship the defective item back** when it can't be refurbished — wastes their time and shipping money
- **Requiring proof** ("Please send a photo before we can help") — customers feel accused; ask AFTER you've committed to fixing
- **Delaying replacement** for an investigation — replace first, investigate later
- **Boilerplate apologies** ("We're sorry for any inconvenience") — defects warrant real apologies
- **Blaming the manufacturer / fulfillment** ("Our supplier sometimes...") — kills brand credibility; customer bought from the brand, brand owns it
- **Not flagging patterns internally** — replying-once-then-forgetting means the next 10 customers get the same defect

## Public-complaint risk handling

If the ticket includes any of these signals, flag as "public complaint risk":

- Customer mentions Trustpilot, BBB, Google Reviews, or social media
- Customer says "I'll be telling [influencer / friends / network] about this"
- Customer cc's a journalist or named person at the brand
- Customer threatens chargeback
- Customer's prior reviews show a willingness to complain publicly

Recommended actions for high-risk tickets:

- Escalate to founder for personal response
- Over-deliver on resolution (full refund + replacement + extra credit, regardless of what's strictly owed)
- Reply within 4 hours (not the standard 24)
- Followup proactively at 24h and 7d post-resolution to confirm satisfaction

A $50 over-delivery prevents a $5,000 reputational hit. The math is rarely close.

## Auto-detected systemic issues

Defects category is the richest for systemic-issue detection. The batch mode's Sheet 3 flags:

- **Same product, same defect, 3+ tickets in 30 days:** likely manufacturing batch issue
- **Same component failure pattern (e.g., handle breaking):** design issue, not a one-off
- **Same fulfillment region with BOA reports:** packaging issue at that region
- **Same supplier-batch identifier in multiple complaints:** isolate that batch immediately
- **Increasing complaint rate over time (week-over-week):** quality declining; investigate

These show as actionable recommendations in the brief — "investigate batch X" with the supporting ticket list.
